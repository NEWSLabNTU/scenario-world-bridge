# Your car

Drive a scenario with another car than the default Tesla Model 3: a car that ships, or one
of your own from its vehicle spec. One spec describes the car; the simulators' physics and
sensors, SSv2's catalog entry and Autoware's vehicle packages are all made from it, and a
run whose parts came from different specs is refused ([the spec check](#the-spec-check)).

## What is built and what is not

| | State |
|---|---|
| the two shipped cars, `tesla_model3` and `drift_mustang` | built and installed: name them in the scenario and in `swb.yaml` |
| the spec's physics on CARLA | works, given to the world server through `stack_args` (below); no project key |
| a car of your own: catalog, CARLA physics and sensors, the Isaac rig | generated from your spec by the commands below |
| a car of your own: Autoware's packages (`<car>_description`, `<car>_sensor_kit_*`) | generated by `autoware_sim_vehicle`'s generator, built in a workspace of yours; Autoware only |
| a car of your own: pedal maps (throttle and brake to acceleration) | measured, not generated; nothing in these pages says how yet |
| `vehicles:` in `swb.yaml` | checked by `project check` (the file must exist), not loaded by the stack: give the stack your files through `stack_args` |

Every limit in one place: [Supported and not supported](../reference/limits.md#projects).

## A car that ships

Two cars ship: `tesla_model3` (the default) and `drift_mustang`. The car of an entity is
named twice, and the two must agree (`project check` says so when they do not):

- in the scenario, the entity's catalog entry: `<CatalogReference catalogName="vehicles"
  entryName="drift_mustang">`;
- in `swb.yaml`, the pilot's car: `ego: {kind: autoware, vehicle: drift_mustang}`.

On CARLA, the car's physics come from its spec only when the world server is given its
`carla_physics.yaml`; without it, CARLA's blueprint keeps its own mass, engine and tyres.
Pass it through the stack; it is merged with what the stack gives the world server itself
(the state directory, the ports):

```bash
G=$(ros2 pkg prefix --share sim_vehicle_spec)/generated
play_launch launch --web-addr 127.0.0.1:8080 \
    swb_launch swb.launch.xml \
    stack_args:="sim_args:='vehicle_physics:=$G/drift_mustang/carla_physics.yaml'"
```

The pilot's vehicle bridge then reads the physics back from CARLA and checks them against
the spec.

## A car of your own

Start from a spec, `vehicles/<car>.yaml` in your project. The format is
[sim_vehicle_spec's spec format](https://github.com/NEWSLabNTU/sim_vehicle_spec/blob/main/docs/spec-format.md);
the two shipped specs are worked examples to copy
(`$(ros2 pkg prefix --share sim_vehicle_spec)/vehicles/tesla_model3.yaml`). The car's
body in CARLA is a CARLA vehicle blueprint (`body.catalogue_model`, for example
`vehicle.lincoln.mkz_2020`): swb does not import a 3D model of its own, and the body must
not be one a shipped car already uses.

`sim_vehicle_spec.gen` writes what the bridge and CARLA need:

```bash
python3 -m sim_vehicle_spec.gen \
    --spec vehicles/my_car.yaml \
    --catalog catalogs/vehicles.xosc \
    --spec-hash config/spec_hash.yaml \
    --carla carla
```

| Output | Used by |
|---|---|
| `catalogs/vehicles.xosc` | the scenario's `CatalogLocations`, and the bridge (`vehicle_catalogs`) |
| `config/spec_hash.yaml` | the vehicle agent (`spec_hash_file`) |
| `carla/carla_physics.yaml` | CARLA's world server (`vehicle_physics`) |
| `carla/carla_sensors.yaml` | the CARLA vehicle bridge's sensors |

Give the bridge your catalog, and the world server your physics, through `stack_args`:

```bash
play_launch launch --web-addr 127.0.0.1:8080 \
    swb_launch swb.launch.xml \
    stack_args:="vehicle_catalogs:=$PWD/catalogs/vehicles.xosc \
sim_args:='vehicle_physics:=$PWD/carla/carla_physics.yaml'"
```

**With Autoware**, the car also needs its own Autoware packages, `<car>_description` (with
its `config/spec_hash.yaml` and the pedal maps) and `<car>_sensor_kit_*`, built in a
workspace you source in `~/swb.bash` after this one. They are generated from the spec by
`python3 -m autoware_gen` of the
[autoware_sim_vehicle](https://github.com/NEWSLabNTU/autoware_sim_vehicle) repository, and
the pedal maps are measured on the car, not generated. The pilot then names the car, and
Autoware loads its packages by that name:

```yaml
vehicles: [vehicles/my_car.yaml]
pilots:
  ego: {kind: autoware, vehicle: my_car}
```

**Without Autoware**, none of Autoware's packages is needed, but CARLA's vehicle bridge
still waits for the car's description on `/robot_description` and places the sensors at its
frames: your launch publishes a URDF of the car with `base_link` and a frame per sensor of
the spec ([Your autopilot, the vehicle interface](your-autopilot.md#what-carlas-vehicle-bridge-waits-for)).
The pedal maps matter here too: CARLA's vehicle bridge turns the commanded acceleration
into throttle and brake through them.

On Isaac the car is an aib ego config (`rig:`, `body:`) inside the world server, and
`aib.gen` writes the rig from the spec (`python3 -m aib.gen --spec vehicles/my_car.yaml -o
isaac/my_car.rig.yaml`); the world server takes it as `ego:=<config>` in its `sim_args`.

## The spec check

Before the ego starts, the bridge compares the `spec_hash` of each part: the world
server's body (on CARLA only when it applied a `carla_physics.yaml`), the vehicle agent's,
and the catalog entry of the ego's body. When two differ the spawn fails:

```
Spawn 'ego': the ego's parts were built from different vehicle specs (the world
server (...) 480be4fd0d00, the vehicle agent 65f1ba86a28f): rebuild and restart the
stale one
```

Make all three from one spec; after changing a spec, run the generators again, rebuild,
and restart the stack. Two related refusals:

- `the ego's body on ... is not the car SSv2 declared (the vehicle catalog's), more than
  0.02 m off`: the scenario's catalog entry and the simulator's body are different cars.
- `vehicle catalogs disagree on <body>`: your catalog gives a body that the installed
  catalog already uses (`vehicle.tesla.model3`, `vehicle.ford.mustang`) another spec. Give
  your car a body of its own.
