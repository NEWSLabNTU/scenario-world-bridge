# Your car

Drive a scenario with another car than the default Tesla Model 3: a car that ships, or one
of your own from its vehicle spec. One spec describes the car; the simulators' physics and
sensors, SSv2's catalog entry and Autoware's vehicle packages are all made from it, and a
run whose parts came from different specs is refused ([the spec check](#the-spec-check)).

## What is built and what is not

| | State |
|---|---|
| the two shipped cars, `tesla_model3` and `drift_mustang` | built and installed: name them in the scenario and in `testbench.yaml` |
| the spec's physics on CARLA | applied by the stack, to a shipped car and to an own car, on every car of the pilot's model; `vehicle_physics:=none` keeps CARLA's blueprint (below) |
| a car of your own on CARLA, any pilot kind but `autoware` | listed in `vehicles:`: the stack makes everything from the spec (below) |
| a car of your own with Autoware | also needs Autoware's packages (`<car>_description`, `<car>_sensor_kit_*`), generated by `autoware_sim_vehicle`'s generator and built in a workspace of yours |
| a car of your own: pedal maps (throttle and brake to acceleration) | measured, not generated; without them the car drives on the Tesla Model 3's |
| a car of your own on Isaac | listed in `vehicles:`: the stack makes its Isaac rig from the spec (`state/vehicles/<car>/isaac/<car>.rig.yaml`) and drives it as the ego; it shows as its chassis box. A 1/10 car is worked through in `scenarios/projects/roboracer` (roadmap 091 W6 to W9) |

Every limit in one place: [Supported and not supported](../reference/limits.md#testbenches).

## A car that ships

Two cars ship: `tesla_model3` (the default) and `drift_mustang`. The car of an entity is
named twice, and the two must agree (`testbench check` says so when they do not):

- in the scenario, the entity's catalog entry: `<CatalogReference catalogName="vehicles"
  entryName="drift_mustang">`;
- in `testbench.yaml`, the pilot's car: `ego: {kind: autoware, vehicle: drift_mustang}`.

On CARLA, the stack gives the world server the car's `carla_physics.yaml` (a shipped car's
from `sim_vehicle_spec`), so the car runs its spec's mass, engine and tyres, and so does
every other car of the same model, a vehicle agent's too. Before 2026-11-16 a shipped car
kept CARLA's blueprint's. To run the blueprint's own physics, or another file, pass it
to a stack started in the foreground; it is merged with what the stack gives the world
server itself (the state directory, the ports):

```bash
ros2 launch scensim_launch scensim.launch.xml testbench:=my_testbench \
    stack_args:="sim_args:='vehicle_physics:=none'"
```

With the spec's physics, the pilot's vehicle bridge reads them back from CARLA and checks
them against the spec.

## A car of your own

Start from a spec, `vehicles/<car>.yaml` in your testbench. The format is
[sim_vehicle_spec's spec format](https://github.com/NEWSLabNTU/sim_vehicle_spec/blob/main/docs/spec-format.md);
the two shipped specs are worked examples to copy
(`$(ros2 pkg prefix --share sim_vehicle_spec)/vehicles/tesla_model3.yaml`). The car's
name is the spec's `name:`, and it may not be a shipped car's. Its body in CARLA is a
CARLA vehicle blueprint (`body.catalogue_model`, for example `vehicle.lincoln.mkz_2020`):
scensim does not import a 3D model of its own, the body must not be one a shipped car already
uses, and the spec's geometry must be that body's (the bridge refuses an ego more than
2 cm off its catalog entry).

List it in `testbench.yaml` and give it to a pilot:

```yaml
vehicles: [vehicles/my_car.yaml]
pilots:
  ego: {kind: simple, vehicle: my_car}
```

and name it in the scenario, from the catalog the stack makes:

```xml
<CatalogLocations>
  <VehicleCatalog><Directory path="$(env SCENSIM_VEHICLES)" /></VehicleCatalog>
</CatalogLocations>
...
<CatalogReference catalogName="vehicles" entryName="my_car" />
```

When the stack starts, it makes from each listed spec, into `state/vehicles/<car>/`: the
car's catalog entry, its `spec_hash.yaml`, its CARLA physics and sensors, and a URDF of its
frames. One catalog, `state/vehicles/catalogs/vehicles.xosc`, holds the shipped cars and
yours: `$(env SCENSIM_VEHICLES)` names its directory (without own cars, the shipped
catalog's), and the bridge reads it. On CARLA the world server applies the ego's physics,
and the pilot's vehicle bridge gets the car's sensors, its spec_hash and its frames, so
the spec check below passes with no Autoware package of the car. `testbench check` names a
taken name or body.

A spec can say more than a simulator applies. CARLA has no physics material per actor, so it
leaves out a spec's `body.contact` (the body's restitution and friction against walls and
objects), and CARLA 1.0 keeps some tyre and suspension values from the blueprint. Nothing is
dropped silently:
- `testbench check` notes a `body.contact` on a CARLA testbench;
- the stack's start names every left-out field for the car on that CARLA;
- each run's `result.json` keeps the list (`provenance.not_applied`).

Isaac applies them all, front and rear tyres (`tires.front`, `tires.rear`) included.

The pedal maps (acceleration to throttle and brake) are measured on the car, not
generated: put `accel_map.csv` and `brake_map.csv` in `vehicles/<car>_maps/` beside the
spec. Without them the car drives on the Tesla Model 3's, and `stack.json` records that.

**With Autoware**, the car also needs its own Autoware packages, `<car>_description` (with
its `config/spec_hash.yaml` and the pedal maps) and `<car>_sensor_kit_*`, built in a
workspace you source in `~/scensim.bash` after this one. They are generated from the spec by
`python3 -m autoware_gen` of the
[autoware_sim_vehicle](https://github.com/NEWSLabNTU/autoware_sim_vehicle) repository, and
`testbench check` refuses an `autoware` pilot of the car until they are installed.

**Your own launch** (`launch` or `command` with a vehicle side) gets the car's files as
launch arguments (`vehicle_config`, `spec_hash_file`, `autoware_spec_hash_file`,
`vehicle_maps_dir`); publishing the car's description on `/robot_description` stays your
launch's ([Your autopilot, the vehicle interface](your-autopilot.md#what-carlas-vehicle-bridge-waits-for)).

## The spec check

Before the ego starts, the bridge compares the `spec_hash` of each part: the world
server's body (on CARLA only when it applied a `carla_physics.yaml`), the vehicle agent's,
and the catalog entry of the ego's body. When two differ the spawn fails:

```
Spawn 'ego': the ego's parts were built from different vehicle specs (the world
server (...) 480be4fd0d00, the vehicle agent 65f1ba86a28f): rebuild and restart the
stale one
```

Make all three from one spec; after changing a spec, run the generators again, rebuild,
and restart the stack. Two related refusals:

- `the ego's body on ... is not the car SSv2 declared (the vehicle catalog's), more than
  0.02 m off`: the scenario's catalog entry and the simulator's body are different cars.
- `vehicle catalogs disagree on <body>`: your catalog gives a body that the installed
  catalog already uses (`vehicle.tesla.model3`, `vehicle.ford.mustang`) another spec. Give
  your car a body of its own, in `body:` and in each of `bodies:` (a copy of a shipped spec
  keeps the shipped car's there); `testbench check` names each one.
