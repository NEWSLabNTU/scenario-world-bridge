# Tilt the road, load the tyres, price the edge

The published answer to all three questions converges on one structure, and the autoware-drift race planner has only the first third of it. Minimum-time research treats the road as a ribbon with three angles along s (heading, slope, bank) and turns gravity and the ribbon's rotation into **apparent accelerations plus an apparent vertical acceleration g~** that scales every tyre limit. The term that matters most is not bank but **vertical curvature times speed squared**: dropping it cost 3.3 s of a 120 s Bathurst lap and let the plan exceed the true grip limit by **12.6 m/s^2** ([Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)). Bank enters twice, as a lateral share of gravity and through g~, and a crowned road gives the line a camber transition every time it crosses the centre. Games split the road the same way the planner should: physics reads a dense mesh with a material per triangle, while the AI reads a sparse line carrying camber, grade and several edge widths, yet no documented game stores what lies beyond the edge, and the AI usually ignores even the camber it stores. Edges are the least modelled and best measured part: road-safety tests show that the **lip on re-entry, not the excursion, is the hazard** (a 4.5 in vertical edge is unsafe even at 35 mph), that gravel decelerates at **0.3 to 1.1 g**, and that a 1V:3H foreslope, which is swb's roadmap 066 default, is traversable but not recoverable. The recommendation is one road-section record per station and side, written by swb's course builder and read by Isaac, the planner and the test plant alike, carrying bank, crown, vertical curvature and an edge hazard class; then g~ and bank terms in L1, L2 and the plant; then a margin, weight and style gate per hazard class, with off-road outcomes scored by class.

## Vertical curvature costs more seconds than bank

The modern formulation descends from Lot and Biral's curvilinear 3D road model, which changes the independent variable from time to distance along the road ([Lot and Biral 2014](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/ifac2014/media/files/0868.pdf)), and is laid out in Limebeer and Warren's review. The road frame is R = Rz(theta) Ry(mu) Rx(phi), with theta the heading, mu the slope and phi the bank, and its rates per metre are the geodesic curvature (in-plane turning), the **normal curvature Omega_y** (crests and dips) and the relative torsion Omega_x (bank changing along the road). The standard ribbon is "laterally flat": camber does not vary across the road ([Limebeer and Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)). The review names the physical effect plainly: tyres lose force-generating capacity at the summit of a hill, gain it in a dip, and "adverse camber [is] particularly troublesome at high speed."

Rowold, Ögretmen and colleagues at TUM give the version that runs online. A point mass in the velocity frame is constrained by a g-g diagram evaluated at three apparent quantities, a~x, a~y and g~, where g~ = w_dot - omega_y V + (omega_x^2 - omega_y^2) h + g cos(mu) cos(phi), and the gravity shares in a~x and a~y depend on slope, bank and the angle chi between the velocity and the road ([Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)). Their ablation is the clearest evidence in the literature of which terms earn their cost:

| term dropped | Bathurst lap (s) | Las Vegas lap (s) | worst violation of the true g-g limit (m/s^2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 119.893 | 27.116 | 0 |
| h times d(omega)/dt | 119.862 | 27.116 | 0.78 / 0.12 |
| h times omega times omega | 119.913 | 27.116 | 0.22 |
| w_dot in g~ | 120.169 | 27.126 | 3.55 / 0.02 |
| **omega_y V in g~** | **123.181** | **29.512** | **12.55 / 1.40** |
| whole track flattened | 124.265 | 31.876 | not reported |

The omega_y V term "accounts for varying downforces due to banked turns and at apexes of hills and dips," grows with speed, and is the one a hill course cannot do without; the w_dot term mattered at Bathurst only because alternating turns flip the banking quickly ([Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)). The online planner ran at about 80 ms with acados over a 300 to 500 m horizon after the negligible terms were removed, and its local lines reproduced the global lap time to 7 ms. Lovato and Massaro built the same idea into quasi-steady-state solvers as g-g-g diagrams, whose third axis is vertical acceleration ([Padova record](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3375385)). The exception to the laterally flat ribbon is Darlington: with a flat ribbon the optimal NASCAR line ran around the bottom of the track, and only a curved ribbon, with camber varying across the road, put it against the wall where real cars run ([Limebeer and Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download); [Lovato, Massaro, Limebeer 2021](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11012-021-01387-3)). That exception matters here, because a crowned mountain road is a curved ribbon.

For a car roughly aligned with the road, the equations reduce to three lines. In the planner's own sign convention (grade positive climbing, theta = atan(grade), n and kappa positive to the left) and with bank phi defined positive when the road leans left, so that kappa times phi greater than zero means on-camber, the tyres must supply a~x = a_x + g sin(theta) along the road and a~y = a_y - g cos(theta) sin(phi) across it, against a normal acceleration **g~ = g cos(theta) cos(phi) + V^2 (kappa_v cos(phi) + kappa sin(phi))**, where kappa_v = d(theta)/ds is positive in a sag and negative over a crest. This is a derivation from Rowold's equation 10 and the Limebeer frame, not a quoted formula. Its size on a touge is large. At 20 m/s over a crest of 100 m vertical radius, g~ falls to 0.59 g, and the car leaves the ground at V = sqrt(g R_v), 31 m/s for a 100 m crest and 22 m/s for a 50 m one; the same sag gives 1.41 g. A 4 % crown gives only 0.04 g of lateral gravity, but in a 20 m hairpin at mu = 1 the bank-aware limit v^2 = g R (mu + tan phi) / (1 - mu tan phi) moves the corner speed by about 4 % up on the on-camber half and 4 % down on the off-camber half, an 8 % swing across a road the line crosses at every apex.

Two results temper this. Subosits and Gerdes found that model fidelity changed the optimal line and lap time little, while control needed the richer model ([Stanford CARS summary](https://cars.stanford.edu/news/model-fidelity-and-trajectory-planning-autonomous-vehicles-driving-limits)), and no primary paper on drifting on banked or off-camber roads was found; the drift case below is derived, not measured. The data side also has a trap: because the curvature term multiplies V^2, noise in the elevation profile becomes phantom crests exactly where the car is fastest, and both the review and TUM smooth the track by an optimal-control fit before planning ([Limebeer and Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)). Off-road MPC work does the same to scanned terrain ([Baxter et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20525v1)).

## Games store camber but drive on recorded speed

Every documented title separates physics from AI. Assetto Corsa's AI line stores per point a position, speed, gas, brake, radius, left and right side widths, **camber, direction, surface normal and grade** ([actools AiPointExtra.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/e5e5c94b8dee7511def0b670bfd5dbb233831c11/AcTools/AiFile/AiPointExtra.cs)), yet the only public consumer, AssettoServer's traffic AI, sets corner speed from radius alone and uses camber only to roll the rendered car; grade is read and discarded ([AssettoServer AiState.cs](https://github.com/compujuckel/AssettoServer/blob/main/AssettoServer/Server/Ai/AiState.cs)). Commercial AI handles elevation implicitly through the speeds recorded on a hot lap. Turn 10's racing-line patent says outright that "height information is ignored" ([US7892078B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7892078)). GT Sophy took the opposite route and learned it: its observation holds 60 3D points along each edge and the centreline over about the next 6 s, "the local course surface inclination", and the load on each tyre, with no hand-written crest or bank rule ([Wurman et al. 2022](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/nature22.pdf)). The earlier GTS agent saw no elevation at all and was still superhuman on its tracks ([Fuchs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07971)), which says flat-ish circuits forgive the omission; a touge does not.

The edge side of game data is richer than the 3D side. rFactor 2's AI corridors carry four widths per waypoint according to community documentation: road edge, the far driveable edge including run-off, the collision edge before a barrier, and the corner-cut edge, and track builders taper them by hand to shape behaviour ([rF2 AIW tutorial](https://www.studio-397.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/rF2_AIW_Creation_Tutorial.pdf); [racesimcentral](https://www.community.racesimcentral.net/threads/aiw-file-editing.4795)). Physics surfaces carry the material: rF2 grass has grip 0.66 against road 1.0, rolling resistance 600 against 0, bumps of 49 mm against 2 mm, and `Legal false` ([Studio 397 TDF](https://docs.studio-397.com/x/lgFDAg)); BeamNG's ground models add depth and non-Newtonian flow for mud ([BeamNG groundmodels](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/levels/level_formats/groundmodels/)). Split friction then falls out of each wheel sampling its own surface. GT Sophy's paper names the gap that remains: humans "could see the kerbs and surface material outside the boundaries, whereas GT Sophy could only sense these by driving on them" ([Wurman et al. 2022](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/nature22.pdf)). No title found stores a hazard class beyond the edge.

How game AI prices leaving the road is the most borrowable part. Fixed penalties failed for Fuchs et al.: the policy either ignored them or stood still, and only a **wall cost proportional to speed squared** while in contact stopped it from grinding along walls ([Fuchs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07971)). GT Sophy used the same kinetic-energy form for walls and for time off course, masked progress while off course to remove the incentive to cut, and counted the car off only with three or more tyres out, because kerbs and tarmac beyond the line are often in bounds ([Wurman et al. 2022](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/nature22.pdf)). The GameAIPro architecture adds a recovery behaviour triggered by an integrated utility, at 10 Hz adding 10 points per metre off the track and per wheel without grip and subtracting 20 per frame, then driving to the nearest edge at modest speed after first stopping the slide ([Tomlinson and Melder](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter38_An_Architecture_Overview_for_AI_in_Racing_Games.pdf)).

Rally pace notes are the human encoding of all three topics, and they map almost token for token onto course annotations: crest and small crest, "over crest" (a crest inside a corner, where load drops while lateral demand peaks), "adverse camber", "tightens" and "opens", "don't cut" for a hazard on the inside, and "keep in" or "keep middle" as a lateral corridor ([Wikipedia: Pacenotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacenotes); [racemarket.net](https://racemarket.net/blog/rally-pacenotes-what-why-how)). Coaching gives the matching rules: on-camber acts as banking, off-camber needs earlier and smoother braking and sometimes an early apex to use the on-camber part, a crown crossing at the limit behaves "almost like a crest", and camber transitions cause more crashes than drivers expect ([Team O'Neil](https://teamoneil.com/blog/road-camber-explained/)); over a crest come off the power slightly and feed it back on landing ([Mazda Motorsports](https://www.mazdamotorsports.com/2020/05/11/kenton-koch-on-driving-technical-corners/)); and for jumps, set the attitude before take-off and choose take-off speed by what the landing zone allows ([auto123 / wrc.com](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/rally-how-to-jump-a-world-rally-car/17964)).

## The lip on re-entry, not the excursion, is the hazard

Road-safety testing explains why two wheels off is survivable and the return is not. When a wheel drops off a pavement edge and the driver steers gently back, the front tyre meets the edge at a flat angle and scrubs along it, held by the lip, until the steer angle reaches a critical value; then "in less than one wheel revolution, the pavement edge force has disappeared and the cornering force of the right front wheel may have doubled," and the car yaws across the road ([Ivey](https://saferroadsconference.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Don-Ivey-Innovations-in-Asphaltic-Pavement-Edge-Profiles-to-Improve-Safety-and-Reduce-Maintenance-Costs.pdf)). Zimmer and Ivey's tests found **a 4.5 in vertical edge potentially unsafe even at 35 mph**, with severity rising nearly linearly with return speed, while a 45 degree edge kept even a 6 in drop within the safe zone ([TTI 328-1](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedPDFs/tti/328-1.pdf)). A 45 degree taper cuts a 4 in edge's effective height to about 1 in, and a 30 degree edge is about 60 % as severe as a 45 degree one, which is why FHWA adopted the 30 degree Safety Edge ([Ivey](https://saferroadsconference.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Don-Ivey-Innovations-in-Asphaltic-Pavement-Edge-Profiles-to-Improve-Safety-and-Reduce-Maintenance-Costs.pdf)). Crash data put the risk threshold at about 2.5 in, and the recovery advice is to slow to 30 mph or less while straddling the edge before steering back, because braking on the split surface can itself lose control ([Iowa LTAP 2006](https://cwims.intrans.iastate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/edge_drop-off.pdf)).

What lies beyond the shoulder decides the rest. Gravel arrester beds give passenger cars about **0.3 g coasting and up to 0.9 g braking** ([Ambrož et al. 2020](https://mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/5/1761)), about 1 g with any sideways component ([FISITA ICRASH 2002](https://go.fisita.com/store/papers/ICRASH2002/PAPER75)), and the FIA measured an average 1.1 g in Schumacher's 1999 Silverstone trap ([AtlasF1](https://archive.atlasf1.com/news/1999/1438.htm)): a predictable decelerator of known length. Foreslopes come in three AASHTO classes: **1V:4H or flatter is traversable and recoverable, between 1V:3H and 1V:4H traversable but not recoverable** (the car reaches the toe), and steeper than 1V:3H not traversable ([FHWA clear zone](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/programadmin/clearzone.cfm)). A ditch on a rally stage is the extreme case of non-recoverable: Rally Poland cars that slid in needed spectators to push them out ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/watch-this/9430/watch-these-beautiful-rally-cars-crash-into-a-ditch-one-by-one.md)). Physical deterrents also beat rules: Red Bull Ring added a 2.5 m gravel strip behind a kerb because "when you run over the gravel, it's impossible to be faster" ([formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/explained-how-key-changes-to-the-red-bull-ring-look-to-avoid-last-years.2ubjMYkbT3HHpwuEJ7PoHw)).

Planning work that prices the consequence by roadside class is thin. Off-road MPC bounds what happens on a slope instead: Han et al. constrain rollover with |h (v^2 kappa + g sin phi)| below the half-track times g cos phi, and bound the pitch torque about the rear axle so a ditch crossing neither launches nor bottoms, which made them up to 22 % faster than geometric baselines at 7 to 10 m/s ([Han et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12284v3)); Baxter et al. use an energy stability margin that allows wheel lift but not rollover ([Baxter et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20525v1)). Autoware's own Boundary Departure Checker grades departures by braking distance into levels and a cost score, without knowing what is past the boundary ([Autoware BDC](https://autowarefoundation.github.io/autoware_universe/main/common/autoware_boundary_departure_checker)). The open gaps are real: no source gives a planner-level tyre force for a wheel climbing a 5 to 10 cm lip, Zimmer and Ivey's critical steer angles live in figures that were not extracted, and no minimum-time planner pricing run-off by class was found. The design below fills the gap with the game penalty forms and the measured decelerations, and treats lip physics as something the plant must reproduce, not something the planner must model.

## The stack today sees grade and nothing else

The planner, the test plant and swb's course builder share the same blind spots, which is convenient: they agree with each other, so experiments in the plant cannot reveal what both omit.

| piece | what it has | what it lacks |
|---|---|---|
| L1 window (`race_planner/window.py`) | grade as gravity along the road, `sin_t = grade / sqrt(1 + grade^2)`, rotated into the body by the heading error xi; edges as an exact penalty, `EDGE_PENALTY = 10` s per metre past width less half the car less a 0.3 m margin, the same on both sides; hard edge rows only at the last node | bank; g~ (loads come from `model.loads`, static `m g` split plus transfers from the tyre-made ax, ay); any knowledge of what is past the edge |
| L2 tracker (`tracker.py`) | the same grade term; `EDGE_MARGIN = 0.1` m inside the edge | the same |
| styles (`style.py`) | S1 fastest, S2 drift budget, S3 drift max; edge margins are invariants a style cannot loosen | a way to forbid a drift beside a drop |
| test plant (`sim.py`, `live.py`) | the planner's own dynamics with the grade term; ends the run `off_road` 0.5 m past either edge (`live.py` near line 619) | bank, g~, split friction, any outcome but one |
| track record (`track.py`) | s, x, y, z, psi, kappa, w_left, w_right, mu, grade, sliding | bank, crown, vertical curvature, edge class |
| swb course (`course/centreline.py`, `geometry.py`, `props.py`) | z from the DEM, 25 m median then 10 m Gaussian; curvature and grade exported; a ribbon level across; guardrails where the ground 3 m beyond the half width falls more than 1 m, or outside bends tighter than 30 m | bank in the ribbon; the guardrail rule as data (it decides placement and is then forgotten) |
| roadmap 066 R3 (`road_detail.py`) | a cross-section in road coordinates: 4 % A-crown, 0.5 m shoulder, 1V:3H foreslope, 0.30 m ditch with 0.6 m bottom, 1V:2H backslope, the same for every station of a corridor; R5's tyre contact on it | a section that varies by station and side, an edge lip, any link to the planner |

Three consequences follow. First, the gravity term is already right in body frame, so bank slots in beside it the same way: the road's leftward normal in body coordinates is (sin xi, cos xi), so bank adds dvx += g cos(theta) sin(phi) sin(xi) and dvy += g cos(theta) sin(phi) cos(xi). In a drift xi is large, and the bank's gravity share moves from the lateral to the longitudinal axis, which is the correct behaviour and comes for free. Second, `axle.dynamics` takes loads at the tyre-made accelerations, before gravity is added, which is the specific force a load transfer needs; only the static share is wrong, and multiplying it by g~/g corrects it. Third, R3's default section sits exactly on AASHTO's boundary of traversable and non-recoverable, and R3's own 2 km run at 80 km/h ended **stuck in the ditch at 794 m** after washboard hits (roadmap 066): the ditch is already a trap in Isaac, and the plant's single `off_road` result cannot say so. The 10 m smoothing of z also means crests shorter than a few tens of metres are flattened in both the Isaac ribbon and the planner's record. That keeps them consistent, but a scanned twin would carry sharper crests and needs the smoothing set on curvature, not on z.

One physical gap in the plant matters for edges specifically. The PhysX vehicle finds the ground with a query per wheel and has no tyre collision shape, and R5's tandem cam envelopes obstacles in the wheel's own plane. Neither produces the lateral sidewall force that holds a tyre against a lip at a shallow angle, so an Isaac car will likely climb a vertical edge without scrubbing and without the sudden yaw that makes re-entry dangerous. This is an inference from how the two contact models work, not a measurement, and the first edge experiment below should confirm or refute it.

## Recommendation: one road-section record, three consumers

This section is a proposal for later phase documents, not a description of existing work.

### The record

swb's course builder writes `course/section.npz` beside `centreline.npz`, as arrays over the same 1 m stations, and becomes the single source for Isaac's ribbon, `road_detail`, `props.guardrails`, `Track.from_swb_course` and the test plant. Crest and bank fields are exact for generated roads, derived from the smoothed profile for DEM courses, and fitted by a curvature-penalised smoother for scanned twins.

| field | meaning | consumers |
|---|---|---|
| `bank` | road roll, rad, positive leaning left | ribbon, planner, plant |
| `crown_left`, `crown_right` | cross slope of each half, down and away from the crown line (0.04 for R3's default) | road_detail, planner (as bank varying with n), plant |
| `crown_offset` | lateral position of the crown line, m | the same |
| `kappa_v` | d(theta)/ds, 1/m, positive in a sag; computed from the same smoothed z | planner, plant, pace-note export |
| per side: `shoulder_w`, `shoulder_surface` | paved or gravel shoulder width and surface class | road_detail, planner split friction, plant |
| per side: `lip_h`, `lip_angle` | edge drop-off height (m) and face angle (90 vertical, 30 Safety Edge) | road_detail, plant, planner rule |
| per side: `foreslope`, `ditch_depth`, `runoff_len`, `runoff_surface` | the R3 section terms, now per station | road_detail, classifier |
| per side: `barrier_offset` | lateral distance of a guardrail or wall, or none | props, planner, plant |
| per side: `hazard` | the class below, derived by one classifier from the fields above and the DEM | props, planner, plant, scoring |

The classifier replaces today's inline guardrail rule and keeps its intent (ground more than 1 m down within 3 m, or the outside of a bend tighter than 30 m, gets a rail), but writes the result down. The planner's crown is a smooth function of n, for example phi(s, n) = bank(s) + crown_left(s) sigma(n - c) - crown_right(s) (1 - sigma(n - c)) with sigma a logistic of width about 0.5 m, which is the lightest form of Lovato and Massaro's curved ribbon and keeps IPOPT's derivatives continuous.

### Hazard classes and how each style uses them

| class | defined by | planner (L1 and L2) | S1 fastest | S2 drift budget | S3 drift max | plant outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `shoulder` | flush paved or gravel shoulder within the section | in bounds at the shoulder's mu; a wheel on it puts the whole axle at the lower mu (conservative stand-in for split friction) | may use it | may use it | may use it | continues, split friction per wheel |
| `runoff` | 1V:4H or flatter, length L, surface known | soft, exact penalty plus a speed-squared term, priced as the time lost at 0.3 to 0.9 g | margin m0 | margin m0 | margin m0 | `runoff`: continues on the run-off surface, recoverable, time lost scored |
| `lip` | `lip_h` above about 5 cm with a face steeper than 45 degrees | wheels kept on the pavement on that side (margin m0 plus the lip); if off, re-entry only below a speed cap and above a heading angle | margin m1 | margin m1 | no planned slide toward it | `lip`: re-entry checked; shallow fast re-entry scored `upset` |
| `ditch` | foreslope steeper than 1V:4H into a ditch (R3's 1V:3H default) | exact penalty with a large weight; treated as a stop | margin m1 | no planned slide on that side | no planned slide on that side | `ditched`: stop, a DNF |
| `barrier` | `barrier_offset` within a few metres | exact penalty at the barrier less half the car, speed-squared contact cost (Fuchs, GT Sophy) | margin m1 | no planned slide on that side | reduced grip share on that side | `barrier`: crash, normal impact speed logged |
| `drop` | ground more than 1 m down within 3 m, steeper than 1V:3H, no barrier | hard row with the largest margin; the exact penalty weight high enough to act as hard | margin m2 | no planned slide | no planned slide, grip share capped | `crash`: run ends |

The margins m0 < m1 < m2 should not be guessed. The recommended rule is m = half the car plus a quantile of L2's lateral tracking error for that style, measured from logs: for example the 95th percentile for `runoff`, the 99.9th for `drop`. Today's 0.3 m (L1) and 0.1 m (L2) become the `runoff` case. The issue's wording, "no planned slide (S2, S3) in a corner beside a drop, or a smaller grip share there", becomes the style gate column; the margins stay invariants that styles cannot loosen, as `style.py` already requires.

### Planner equations

L1 and L2 gain three parameters per stage, `bank`, `kappa_v` and the crown terms, plus per-side margins and weights replacing the global `EDGE_PENALTY` and `EDGE_MARGIN`. The dynamics add the bank's gravity share in body frame, as above. The loads scale the static share: `rest_loads` times g~/g, with g~ = g cos(theta) cos(phi) + V s_dot (kappa_v cos(phi) + kappa sin(phi)), V the speed and s_dot the progress rate, while the transfers stay on the tyre-made accelerations. A row g~ at least g_min (for example 0.3 g) forbids planned airtime, since nothing in either model can fly; a crest annotated as a jump would lift it and add a landing constraint on speed. Rowold's ablation says the h times d(omega)/dt and h times omega times omega terms can be left out at first and w_dot only matters where bank flips quickly, as on a crowned road where the line crosses the crown; it should be measured, not assumed. The test plant takes the same terms but evaluates them with the true crown as a function of each wheel's lateral position and samples each wheel's surface separately, so it stays the stricter of the two.

### Isaac and roadmap 066

Isaac needs the geometry, not new equations. `geometry.ribbon` tilts each section by `bank` and the crown from the record, `road_detail` reads its section per station and side from the record instead of one constant `Section`, and the section gains the lip (height and face angle) between carriageway and shoulder. A lateral edge force belongs in R5's corner-force layer if the lip experiment confirms that PhysX's contact does not scrub. The guardrails come from the classifier's `barrier` rows, so a car that leaves the road meets exactly what the planner was told was there.

### Validation experiments

Seven experiments make each step falsifiable. A crest and sag fixture (straight road, vertical radius 50, 100 and 200 m) compares each wheel's load in the plant and in Isaac against g~, and the take-off speed against sqrt(g R_v). A banked skidpad at cross slopes from -6 % to +6 % checks the steady corner speed against the bank-aware limit in both. A Rowold-style ablation on a touge course (the Haruna drift course is the obvious one) runs L1 with and without g~, bank and crown, and reports lap time and the worst tyre over-use the plant measures. A crown-crossing test measures L2's lateral error through hairpins whose line crosses the crown. A parity check samples Isaac's road height and cross slope at the record's stations and requires agreement within 5 mm and 0.1 degree, the same 5 mm 066 already asks of collision, render and lidar. A forced-excursion suite pushes the car off on each class, in the plant and in Isaac, and checks run-off deceleration against the 0.3 to 0.9 g band, ditch entries ending stuck, and lip re-entries at 5, 10 and 15 cm with 90, 45 and 30 degree faces showing scrubbing then a yaw spike above the threshold heights. A margin calibration collects L2's lateral error by style and sets m0, m1 and m2 from its quantiles.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the order of work more than its content. Bank is what the issue names, but crests are where the seconds and the grip violations are, and both fall out of one quantity, g~, that the planner's existing load model can take with a single scale factor. Edges invert the intuition the current planner encodes: a symmetric margin treats leaving the road as the event to avoid, while the measured hazard is what happens on the way back (a lip) or what the car meets beyond (a ditch that holds it, a drop that ends it, gravel that merely slows it). Neither games nor research planners publish a hazard class beyond the edge, so this stack would be ahead of both by writing one down.

The design choice that carries the most weight is the shared record, not any equation. Today the planner, the test plant and the course builder agree because all three are equally blind, and Isaac's guardrails encode a hazard judgement that is thrown away after placement. Making the course builder the only author of bank, crown, vertical curvature and edge class turns every later disagreement into a measurable parity error rather than a silent model gap, and gives roadmap 066's off-road road the entry and exit effects the user asked for with physics in Isaac and consequences in the planner. The main unknown left is the lateral lip force: if PhysX's wheel cannot scrub, Isaac will understate re-entry danger, and the planner's lip rule will be the only thing standing between a fast plan and the failure the road-safety tests describe.
