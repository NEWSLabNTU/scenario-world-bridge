# 3D road shape (bank, grade, vertical curvature) and road-edge transitions for a minimum-time planner and NMPC tracker

Context of the target system (read from the code, not a source): the live planner (`race_planner/window.py`) puts grade in only as gravity along the road, `sin_t = grade/sqrt(1+grade^2)`, `dvx -= G*sin_t*cos(xi)`, `dvy += G*sin_t*sin(xi)`; loads come from `model.loads(car, ax, ay)` (quasi-static transfer at flat g); the track carries `s x y z psi kappa w_left w_right mu grade sliding`, so it has no bank, no vertical curvature and nothing about what lies past an edge (autoware-drift issue 014). Roughness, rocks, crown and berm geometry and the PhysX corner model are covered in `reports/Rocks and road roughness for rally.md` and are not repeated here.

## Q1. How 3D track geometry enters minimum-lap-time OCPs: equations, neglected terms, effect sizes

### Takeaway
The working formulation (Lot and Biral 2014, Perantoni and Limebeer 2015, Lovato and Massaro 2021/22, Rowold et al. 2023) is: a ribbon frame with z-y-x Euler angles (heading theta, slope mu, bank phi) along s; the car's accelerations are turned into "apparent" accelerations (a~x, a~y, g~) by adding the gravity components and the frame's rotation terms; the tyre limits are then a g-g diagram that depends on speed and on g~. The single most important 3D term is the vertical-curvature term (omega_y * V, about V^2 times the pitch rate per metre): dropping it cost 3.3 s of 120 s at Bathurst and 2.4 s of 27 s at Las Vegas and violated the true grip limit by up to 12.6 m/s^2. Ignoring the 3D track entirely changed lap time by 4.4 s (Bathurst) and 4.8 s (Las Vegas, 17.6 %).

### Cited Findings
- Lot and Biral (IFAC World Congress 2014, pp. 7559-7565) introduced the curvilinear-abscissa approach: 3D curvilinear road model, moving-frame derivation of the equations of motion, independent variable changed from time to position along the road, solved by an indirect method; case study a GT car. Full text not retrieved here. — [Search summary of Lot & Biral 2014](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/ifac2014/media/files/0868.pdf)
- Ribbon frame (Limebeer and Warren 2022 review, open access, Vehicle System Dynamics 61(6):1449-1475): the Darboux frame (t, n, m) is R = Rz(theta) Ry(mu) Rx(phi) with theta, mu, phi the "attitude, slope and camber angles"; its angular rates per metre are [Omega_x; Omega_y; Omega_z] = [[1, 0, -s_mu],[0, c_phi, c_mu s_phi],[0, -s_phi, c_mu c_phi]] [phi'; mu'; theta'], where Omega_x is the relative torsion, Omega_y the normal (vertical) curvature and Omega_z the geodesic (in-plane) curvature; the centre line is the integral of t = [c_theta c_mu, s_theta c_mu, -s_mu] ds. "While oriented at a general incline, this road model is assumed laterally flat, i.e. the road camber and pitch do not vary with lateral position." — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- Same review: "a reduction in the tyres' force-generating capacity as a vehicle negotiates the summit of a hill, or conversely, their increased force-generating capability for a vehicle driving through a dip. Similar effects occur in relation to cambered corners, with adverse camber particularly troublesome at high speed." — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- Same review, hierarchy: "Entry-level 3D road models pertain to inclined planar surfaces, which amount essentially to changing the orientation of the gravity vector. After that ribbon-based road models emerged that treat the road as 'laterally flat'"; then curved-ribbon models (lateral curvature) motivated by NASCAR. Road models eliminate the vertical, pitch and roll freedoms for a car without suspension. — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- Perantoni and Limebeer, "Optimal control of a Formula One car on a three-dimensional track", Part 1 (track modelling and identification from noisy GPS data, differential geometry) and Part 2 (optimal control; car on a track described by geodesic and normal curvatures and relative torsion, track treated as a tangent plane under the car; 2D vs 3D compared at Barcelona), ASME J. Dyn. Sys. Meas. Control 137(5), 2015. The Barcelona 2D-vs-3D lap-time numbers were not retrieved. — [Part 2 record](https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/optimal-control-of-a-formula-one-car-on-a-three-dimensional-track-3/); [Part 1 record](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/oxford-uuid:3a7cfbe2-facf-479f-9208-089b1b22b2ae)
- Rowold, Ögretmen, Kasolowsky, Lohmann, "Online Time-Optimal Trajectory Planning on Three-Dimensional Race Tracks", IEEE IV 2023 (arXiv 2304.10954). Point mass in a velocity frame V, state x = [V, n, chi_hat, a_hat_x, a_hat_y], inputs = longitudinal and lateral jerk; s_dot = V cos(chi_hat)/(1 - n Omega_z); vertical velocity w = n omega_x. The apparent accelerations that the g-g diagram constrains (eq. 10, h = CoM height):
  - a~x = a_hat_x + d(omega_hat_y)/dt h - omega_hat_x omega_hat_z h + g (c_mu s_phi s_chi - s_mu c_chi)
  - a~y = a_hat_y + d(omega_hat_x)/dt h + omega_hat_y omega_hat_z h + g (s_mu s_chi + c_mu s_phi c_chi)
  - g~ = w_dot - omega_hat_y V + (omega_hat_x^2 - omega_hat_y^2) h + g c_mu c_phi
  - with omega_hat_x = (Omega_x c_chi + Omega_y s_chi) s_dot, omega_hat_y = (Omega_y c_chi - Omega_x s_chi) s_dot.
  — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- Rowold et al. ablation (Table I, global lap time in s, Bathurst MPCB / Las Vegas LVMS, max violation of the true g-g limit in m/s^2): all terms 119.893 / 27.116 (0.00/0.00); drop w*omega terms in the dynamics 119.940 / 27.116; drop the h*d(omega)/dt terms 119.862 / 27.116 (0.78/0.12); drop the h*omega*omega terms 119.913 / 27.116 (0.22); drop omega_hat_y*V in g~ 123.181 / 29.512 (12.55/1.40); drop w_dot in g~ 120.169 / 27.126 (3.55/0.02). Authors: omega_hat_y V "accounts for varying downforces due to banked turns and at apexes of hills and dips. Its magnitude increases with speed"; w_dot matters at Bathurst because of "rapidly changing banking angles due to alternating left and right turns"; negligible terms "are track-dependent". — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- Rowold et al. 3D vs flattened track (Table II): global 3D 119.913 s vs "2D" 124.265 s at MPCB; 27.116 vs 31.876 s at LVMS; the "2D" line was made by setting mu = phi = p_z = 0 (a flattened track, not a 2D plan executed on the 3D track). Banking raises g~ in turns (T1, 9 deg bank); a descent allows more acceleration; "hill apexes like at s = 1000 m shortly before T1 cause a drop in g~ so that the 3D racing line cannot accelerate as much". — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)

Companion 2021 paper covers 3D fixed-trajectory (apex-finding) approaches. Numbers not retrieved. — [Padova record](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3375385); [fixed-trajectory paper](https://research.unipd.it/bitstream/11577/3399341/1/2021%20-%20Three-dimensional%20fixed-trajectory%20approaches%20to%20the%20minimum-lap%20time%20of%20road%20vehicles%20PP%20-%20MM.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a single-track planner with chi_hat small (car aligned with the road), eq. 10 collapses to (inference): a~x ≈ a_x - g sin(mu); a~y ≈ a_y + g cos(mu) sin(phi); g~ ≈ g cos(mu) cos(phi) - V^2 Omega_y, with Omega_y ≈ mu' cos(phi) + theta' cos(mu) sin(phi). So bank contributes twice: a lateral gravity share g cos(mu) sin(phi) (helps when banked into the turn), and through Omega_y a term V^2 kappa sin(phi) that raises g~ in a banked turn (the "bank pushes the car into the road" effect). The present planner already carries the a~x grade part (its sin_t); it lacks the a~y bank part and the whole g~ line.
- Sign check (inference from R = Rz Ry Rx and t_z = -sin mu): mu > 0 is downhill, so a crest (uphill to downhill) has mu' > 0, Omega_y > 0 and lowers g~ by V^2 mu'. At 30 m/s over a crest with 200 m vertical radius, V^2/R = 4.5 m/s^2, so g~ ≈ 0.54 g: the whole g-g diagram shrinks by about half for a load-proportional tyre (less, given tyre load sensitivity). This is the dominant effect a rally/hill course would show, and it is what the 12.6 m/s^2 violation at Bathurst reflects.
- The planner's `grade` field (dz/ds) gives mu; vertical curvature is d(mu)/ds ≈ d(grade)/ds / (1+grade^2). Both can be computed from the existing `z` column without new survey data; bank needs a new column.
- For axle loads in `axle.py`: with g~ replacing g, the static loads scale by g~/g and the transfer terms use a~x, a~y (inference consistent with Rowold's g-g generation, which runs the double-track model at the apparent accelerations and g~).

### Gaps
- Perantoni-Limebeer Barcelona 2D-vs-3D lap-time delta, Lovato-Massaro Mugello/Barcelona numbers, and the content of Subosits-Gerdes ACC 2015 were not retrieved (paywalled). No retrieved source gives Laguna Seca corkscrew or Nürburgring 3D-vs-2D numbers.
- No Laurense/Gerdes or Fork/Vilà paper on 3D roads was found in this pass.

## Q2. Road ribbon representation: data, smoothing, what a planner needs

### Takeaway
The road is a centre-line ribbon: position plus three Euler angles (theta, mu, phi) as functions of s, from which the curvatures (geodesic, normal, torsion) are derived; it is identified from survey/GPS/LiDAR data by solving a smoothing OCP that penalises the rate of change of curvature, because a noisy track model makes the downstream minimum-time OCP fail numerically. A planner needs per s: grade (mu), bank (phi), their first derivatives (normal curvature, torsion), heading curvature, widths, and for bank-changing tracks also the rate of bank change (feeds w_dot).

### Cited Findings
- 2D centre-line identification as an OCP: minimise J = integral (r u^2 + (x_i - x)^2 + (y_i - y)^2) ds where u is the curvature rate; "The weighting r can be used to low-pass filter the estimate"; closure boundary conditions on heading and position "'immunise' the track modelling problem from noise in the survey data as well as integrator drift". — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- 3D: state carries position, integrator chains for each Euler angle, a road-width variable and (curved ribbon) polynomial coefficients for the cross-section m(s, n) fitted to LiDAR survey points; "It is important that the track model is sufficiently smooth, because it is passed subsequently to a minimum-time OCP and a noise track model may cause numerical issues there." — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- TUM: "We solve an optimization problem, as described in [13], to smooth the resulting track data and ensure continuity at the start-finish line"; track smoothing code ships with the online 3D planner. — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- TUM's sampling-based 3D local planner stores gg-diagrams as lookup tables over (v, g~) interpolated online; code at github.com/TUMRT/sampling_based_3D_local_planning. — [Ögretmen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18643v3.pdf)
- Off-road practice: the scanned (UAV photogrammetry) terrain was "artificially smoothed" before being given to the MPC, because both vehicle models rely on smoothness assumptions. — [Baxter et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20525v1)

### Inferences
- Because the vertical-curvature term multiplies V^2, its noise is amplified most where the car is fastest; the smoothing weight should be set on mu' (and phi') rather than on z. A practical check (inference): plot V^2 mu' along the course; spikes that are not real crests are smoothing failures.
- swb's course builder generates the road analytically (inference from the roadmap context), so mu, phi and their derivatives can be exported exactly rather than estimated; the smoothing problem then only applies to scanned courses (NTU twin).
- For a laterally varying cross-section (crown, off-camber shoulder), the curved-ribbon m(s, n) form is the published representation; the review says it matters on Darlington-like tracks but not most tracks.

### Gaps
- No source gave numeric smoothing weights or acceptable noise levels for mu' or phi'.

## Q3. Effective gravity and g-g(-g) diagrams on 3D roads

### Takeaway
Grip limits scale with the apparent vertical acceleration g~: crests shrink the g-g diagram, dips and banked turns expand it (g~ = 2g is reachable on ovals), less so for drive (positive a_x), which is power-limited. TUM represents this with a diamond-shaped under-approximation (four parameters per (V, g~)), interpolated online.

### Cited Findings
- "An increased vertical acceleration of g~ = 2g, which can readily be achieved in banked turns on oval race tracks, induces an expansion of the gg-diagram, albeit to a lesser extent for positive longitudinal accelerations." — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- Diamond g-g: a~x <= a_x,max(V, g~); |a~y| <= a_y,max(V, g~); |a~x| <= |a_x,min(V, g~)| (1 - (|a~y|/a_y,max)^p)^(1/p), with 1 <= p <= 2 (rhombus to circle); fitted p values p(30 m/s, g) = 1.2, p(30, 2g) = 1.6, p(60, g) = 1.7, p(60, 2g) = 1.6. Diagrams generated with a double-track model with longitudinal and lateral load transfer and a simplified Pacejka tyre, maximising combined apparent acceleration at given V, g~ and direction. — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- Cost of the under-approximation: exact polar g-g gave 114.014 s vs 119.913 s at Bathurst (≈6 s), due to missing low-speed traction; no loss at LVMS; the polar form needed more SQP iterations and often exceeded 100 ms. — [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- Lovato and Massaro's g-g-g diagrams (third axis = vertical acceleration) underpin the 3D QSS method; Lovato and Massaro earlier extended velocity-dependent g-g diagrams to depend on g~. — [Padova record](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3375385); [Rowold et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10954.pdf)
- In the sampling-based planner the banking of turn T2 at LVMS "causes an increase in g~" that the planner exploits; when 3D is disabled the checks use a~ = a_hat and g~ = g. — [Ögretmen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18643v3.pdf)

### Inferences
- The present planner already has explicit axle loads and a Pacejka-like tyre rather than a g-g lookup, so it does not need the diamond: feeding g~ into the loads (Fz_static scaled by g~/g, plus transfers from a~x, a~y) and the bank share into the lateral balance gives the same effect with the tyre's own load sensitivity (inference).
- In a car without aero (the target), the g~ dependence is only through tyre load sensitivity and the load transfer; the RWD traction limit scales with rear load, so on a crest the drive limit falls about in proportion to g~ (inference).

### Gaps
- No numeric g-g-g data for a road car without aero was retrieved.

## Q4. Drifting and limit handling on banked or off-camber roads

### Takeaway
Little primary literature was found specifically on drifting on banked roads. The available sourced statement is qualitative: adverse camber is "particularly troublesome at high speed". The physics enters the same way as for grip driving: lateral gravity share and g~.

### Cited Findings
- "Similar effects occur in relation to cambered corners, with adverse camber particularly troublesome at high speed." — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- Off-road minimum-time manoeuvres "are characterised by aggressive, high-drift counter-steering type manoeuvres" (review's summary of prior work on flat roads with varied surfaces). — [Limebeer & Warren 2022](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/c3d40447-9756-4cab-af2a-d7c9502f8fe7/download)
- Roll-including single-track MPC for high-speed AGVs accounting for path curvature and road bank (MDPI Applied Sciences 2018): roll dynamics matter for high CoG or large curvature with road banks. — [Appl. Sci. 8(5):822](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/8/5/822)

### Inferences
- In a slide the car's velocity frame is rotated by the large sideslip beta relative to the body; Rowold's chi_hat = chi + beta already distinguishes them, so the gravity components in eq. 10 should be taken in the velocity frame, which for a drift means the bank's gravity share splits between a~x and a~y by sin/cos of chi_hat (inference). On an off-camber corner (phi negative relative to the turn) the bank share subtracts from the available lateral force and, through Omega_y, lowers g~; the planner's S2/S3 slide styles should see a smaller grip share there.

### Gaps
- No Stanford or Toyota drifting paper on banked roads was found in this pass.

## Q5. Road-edge entry and exit, roadside slopes, and consequence-aware margins

### Takeaway
The road-safety literature quantifies the edge itself: a vertical edge of about 4.5 in (11 cm) is unsafe on re-entry even at 35 mph because the tyre scrubs along the lip until a critical steer angle, then climbs in under one wheel revolution and yaws the car across the road; 45 deg and 30 deg tapered edges cut the effective height (a 4 in edge to about 1 in). Run-off surfaces differ: gravel arrester beds give 0.3 g coasting and up to 0.9 g braking (about 1 g with sideways motion); foreslopes of 1V:4H or flatter are recoverable, 1V:3H to 1V:4H traversable but not recoverable, steeper is critical. Off-road MPC (RSS 2024, arXiv 2026) bounds rollover and ditch crossing with roll/pitch and energy constraints. Planning work that prices the consequence of leaving the road by roadside class is sparse.

### Cited Findings
- Edge scrubbing mechanism: the right wheels drop off; the driver steers gently back; the front tyre meets the edge "at an extremely flat angle and is prevented from moving back"; equilibrium between the cornering force and the edge force; at the critical steer angle the wheel mounts and "in less than one wheel revolution, the pavement edge force has disappeared and the cornering force of the right front wheel may have doubled"; the car yaws about the right rear tyre into the oncoming lane, then collision, spin or roll. — [Ivey, Innovations in Asphaltic Pavement Edge Profiles](https://saferroadsconference.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Don-Ivey-Innovations-in-Asphaltic-Pavement-Edge-Profiles-to-Improve-Safety-and-Reduce-Maintenance-Costs.pdf)
- Zimmer and Ivey (TTI 328-1, 1982): "the 4.5 inch edge height is a potentially unsafe condition, even at the 35 mph speed"; severity rises nearly linearly with return speed; "The effect of edge height was greatly reduced in the 45 degree edge tests. Even the 6 inch edge severity ... was well within the reasonably safe zone"; lateral acceleration after returning above 0.3 g considered excessive (driver-opinion level from Kummer and Meyer); for a rounded Shape B edge, maintenance lines at 2.5 in and 3.5 in. — [Zimmer & Ivey, TTI 328-1](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedPDFs/tti/328-1.pdf)
- A 45 deg edge lowers the "effective" height of a 4 in edge to 1 in (Ivey and Sicking); crossing a 30 deg edge is "roughly 60% of the 'severity' of crossing a 45 deg edge of equal height"; FHWA adopted 30 deg (Safety Edge); the 1983 chart is "somewhat liberal" for a 90 deg edge at 70 mph (Zimmer 1995 tests at 70+ mph). Klein et al. found control losses with edge drops up to 4 in at speeds generally above 30 mph. — [Ivey](https://saferroadsconference.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Don-Ivey-Innovations-in-Asphaltic-Pavement-Edge-Profiles-to-Improve-Safety-and-Reduce-Maintenance-Costs.pdf); [TTI 328-1](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedPDFs/tti/328-1.pdf)
- Iowa (Hallmark and McDonald, 2002-2004 crashes): drop-offs of 2.5 in or more correlated with increased risk; 2.0 in is a conservative repair threshold; the recovery advice is not to steer back at once, slow to 30 mph or less, straddle the edge, then a quarter turn of steering and counter-steer; braking hard on the split surface can lose control because "Different surfaces on pavement and shoulder will result in different skid resistances". — [Iowa LTAP Technology News, Sep-Oct 2006](https://cwims.intrans.iastate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/edge_drop-off.pdf)
- Severity (search-snippet level, not opened): drop-off-related crashes about twice as likely to be fatal (5.8 % vs 3.0 % in Iowa); edge drop-off present in 55 % of 150 fatal rural two-lane crashes in a Georgia study; Ivey et al. place scrubbing at edge heights of 1 to 5 in. — [FHWA Safety Edge pages](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/rwd/provide-safe-recovery/safetyedge/safety-effects-safetyedge); [MwRSF note](https://mwrsf.unl.edu/q&a/attachments/e5e8ac9d774ea4ec9c83c0e6458095d3.pdf)
- Gravel beds: Ambrož, Trajkovski, Kunc (Sustainability 12(5):1761, 2020) measured passenger cars in gravel arrester beds: average deceleration about 0.3 g coasting, up to 0.9 g braking. A FISITA ICRASH 2002 paper: about 1 g if there is at least a small sideways component. FIA (1999, Schumacher, Silverstone): average 1.1 g in the trap; a 2000 press report cites 0.6 g in tests starting only below 50 mph (secondary, conflicting). — [Ambrož et al. 2020](https://mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/5/1761); [FISITA ICRASH2002](https://go.fisita.com/store/papers/ICRASH2002/PAPER75); [AtlasF1 1999](https://archive.atlasf1.com/news/1999/1438.htm); [grandprix.com 2000](https://www.grandprix.com/news/us-investigation-casts-question-mark-over-gravel-traps.html)
- Foreslopes (AASHTO Roadside Design Guide as relayed by FHWA and state DOTs): 1V:4H or flatter traversable and recoverable; between 1V:3H and 1V:4H traversable but non-recoverable (vehicle likely reaches the toe), if smooth and free of fixed objects; steeper than 1V:3H "not considered traversable" and not part of the clear zone; clear-zone width depends on design speed, ADT and slopes. — [FHWA clear zone](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/programadmin/clearzone.cfm); [Kentucky slope flattening](https://safedesign.uky.edu/slope-flattening/); [WSDOT DM 700 excerpt](https://aii.transportation.org:443/Documents/WashingtonState(ExcerptfromDesignManual700-E).pdf)
- Off-road MPC (Han et al., RSS 2024, Polaris RZR, MPPI 10,000 samples, 5 s horizon, ~30 ms): rollover constraint |P3 (v^2 kappa + g sin phi)| < P2 g cos phi (P2 CoM lateral offset to the wheel line, P3 CoM height), rollover risk RR = |v^2 kappa - g sin phi| / cos phi penalised above a tunable RRmax; ditch constraint on residual pitch torque about the rear axle tau = I a2 + P1 v w2 - P3 g sin(theta) - P1 g cos(theta) bounded in [tau_min, tau_max] (airtime above, bump below), keeping "the wheel forces ... always acting in support of the vehicle"; roll and pitch from the elevation map assuming the wheels sit coplanar on it; up to 22 % faster than geometry-based baselines at 7-10 m/s. — [Han et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12284v3)
- Baxter, Epureanu, Jayakumar, Ersal (arXiv 2603.20525, March 2026): energy stability margin U_ESM = ± M g dh (positive while the CoM projects inside the footprint), dh = R (1 - sin(|phi| + phi_bar)) cos(theta), R = sqrt((h+r)^2 + (e/2)^2), phi_bar = atan(2(h+r)/e), so wheel lift-off is allowed but not rollover; rigid body with four spring-damper corners and per-wheel surface normals vs a planar single-track baseline; 9,900 simulated trials at 5-10 m/s and 48 physical; up to 80 Hz on a laptop GPU. — [Baxter et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20525v1)
- Departure severity in production planning: Autoware's Boundary Departure Checker grades departures into levels using braking distance and outputs a score for trajectory cost; road-departure warning research notes severity "strongly depends on features around the roadway" and that departures can be safe and recoverable where traversable shoulders exist; RSAP's Equivalent Fatal Crash Cost Ratio gives per-hazard costs. — [Autoware BDC](https://autowarefoundation.github.io/autoware_universe/main/common/autoware_boundary_departure_checker); [PSU road-departure warning](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/variable-sensitivity-road-departure-warning-system-based-on-stati/); [NAP chapter](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26521/chapter/5)

### Inferences
- Planner vs plant split (inference): the plant (PhysX in Isaac) must reproduce the edge physically (lip height and angle as collision geometry, shoulder friction per wheel), which the rocks report's heightfield approach supports; the planner needs only a per-side, per-stretch class and a few numbers: shoulder mu (for split-mu when two wheels are off), lip height and angle (to forbid shallow-angle re-entry or price it), what lies beyond (runoff with its deceleration, slope class 1:4/1:3/steeper, barrier, drop), and a margin rule per class.
- A useful planner rule from the scrubbing data (inference): re-entry over a lip above ~5-10 cm at a shallow angle is the hazard, not the excursion; so either keep both wheels on the pavement beside such an edge (hard row) or, if off, plan re-entry at low speed and a larger heading angle, and cap post-re-entry lateral acceleration.
- Split-mu when one side leaves the pavement: the yaw moment from unequal longitudinal forces is what the Iowa advice warns about; a single-track model cannot represent it, but can be made conservative by using the lower mu for the whole axle while any wheel of it is off (inference), with the plant carrying the true split.
- A gravel trap is a predictable decelerator (0.3-0.9 g) and a runoff of known length; a 1:3 or steeper slope or a drop is a crash; a barrier is a crash at an angle-dependent severity. Mapping these to (soft penalty, wider margin, hard row) gives issue 014's classes directly (inference).
- Rollover is unlikely for the target car on a paved course but the Han/Baxter constraints become relevant on a ditch foreslope; the planner can bound |v^2 kappa + g sin(phi)| against track/2 over CoM height as a cheap row near steep foreslopes (inference).
