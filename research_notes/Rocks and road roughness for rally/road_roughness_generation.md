# Small-scale road geometry for gravel and forest roads: statistics, generation and representation

Scope: roughness (ISO 8608, IRI), washboard/corrugation, potholes, ruts, crown/camber, berms/windrows, edges, ditches and cross drains (incl. Japanese 林業専用道), and how to put them into PhysX 5 / Omniverse RTX collision, render and lidar geometry. Tire contact models and rigid rocks are out of scope.

## (a1) ISO 8608 road classes, PSD formula, reference values, and where gravel/forest roads fall

### Takeaway
ISO 8608 fits the displacement PSD as Gd(n) = Gd(n0)·(n/n0)^-w, n0 = 0.1 cycles/m, w = 2 by default, with classes A-H spaced by a factor 4 in Gd(n0) (geometric means 16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096, 16384, 65536, 262144 x 10^-6 m^3). Paved roads are A-D; unpaved roads are usually placed in D-H. Measured gravel PSDs do not follow w = 2: they show breaks/peaks (corrugation at ~2 cycles/m). Published, sourced Gd(n0) values for measured gravel or forest roads were not found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- ISO 8608 class table, Gd(n0) at n0 = 0.1 cycles/m, units 10^-6 m^3: A < 32; B 32-128; C 128-512; D 512-2048; E 2048-8192; F 8192-32768; G 32768-131072; H > 131072. Equivalent Gd(Omega0) at Omega0 = 1 rad/m: A < 2, B 2-8, C 8-32, D 32-128, E 128-512, F 512-2048, G 2048-8192, H > 8192 — [Agostinacchio, Ciampa, Olita 2014, Eur. Transp. Res. Rev. 6:267](https://d-nb.info/1100862730/34)
- Unit conversion: G(n) = 2π·G(Ω); for w = 2, Gd(n0) ≈ 16·Gd(Ω0) — [ResearchGate discussion on ISO 8608](https://www.researchgate.net/post/Can-anyone-provide-clarification-regarding-road-profile-classification-by-ISO-8608)
- Generation formula (harmonic superposition, ISO-class form): h(x) = Σ_{i=0..N} sqrt(Δn)·2^k·10^-3·(n0/(i·Δn))·cos(2π·i·Δn·x + φ_i), with Δn = 1/L, n_max = 1/B (B = sampling interval), N = L/B, φ_i uniform on [0, 2π), and k = 3 (class A) ... 9 (class H). The general form is h(x) = Σ sqrt(2·Δn·Gd(i·Δn))·cos(2π·i·Δn·x + φ_i) — [Agostinacchio et al. 2014](https://d-nb.info/1100862730/34)
- Same paper's practical amplitude bands: very good (A-B) h_max ≈ ±15 mm, good (B-C) ±25 mm, average (C-D) ±50 mm, poor (D-E) ±100 mm — [Agostinacchio et al. 2014](https://d-nb.info/1100862730/34)
- ISO 8608:2016 frequency range notes: lower limit need not go below 0.01 cycles/m for on-road vehicles and **0.05 cycles/m for off-road vehicles**; tyre enveloping acts as a low-pass filter giving a recommended upper limit of **10 cycles/m** (0.1 m wavelength) for general on-road vibration work; for vibration a ~**100 mm wide track** (footprint mean) is usually measured; on soft (sandy) off-road surfaces short undulations can be flattened by wheel load, on hard stony surfaces only tyre enveloping filters — [ISO 8608:2016 preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/71202/05b2151f255b44928f80acb897fc0c2c/ISO-8608-2016.pdf)
- Waviness: general roads usually 1.75 < w < 2.25; "the profile of gravel pavement cannot be approximated with the waviness of w = 2"; Múčka found up to 40 % of roads have differing wavebands and 20 % have short-wave unevenness (w > 2) — [Žuraulis et al. 2021, Appl. Sci. 11:7582](https://d-nb.info/1257077627/34)
- Swedish network measurements: w mostly 1.5-3.5, mean ≈ 2.5 — [Múčka 2017 summary, J. Testing & Evaluation](https://store.astm.org/jte20160265.html) (via search abstract)
- Múčka (2017) compared ISO classes used in 27 simulation papers with measured roads and found marked differences; recommended class A as typical good/average road and B-C for low-quality paved surfaces — [Múčka 2017, JTE](https://store.astm.org/jte20160265.html)
- Simulation literature commonly associates classes D-H with unpaved roads — [Múčka, Simulated Road Profiles According to ISO 8608](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320302446_Simulated_Road_Profiles_According_to_ISO_8608_in_Vibration_Analysis)
- Measured gravel road (Lithuania, 160 m straight rural section, three states: PS-1 two days after blading, PS-2 three weeks after, PS-3 worst before maintenance): PSD has a distinct break at **n = 2 cycles/m (0.5 m wavelength)** in all states, PS-3 has an additional break at ~**6.5 cycles/m**; corrugation identified, strongest in PS-3; large loose grains after blading did not matter for vehicle response because of tyre enveloping — [Žuraulis et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1257077627/34)
- Unpaved test courses have been graded against ISO 8608 with a laser/gyro/encoder profilometer (Korea) — [Sensors 7:2004](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/7/9/2004)

### Inferences
- RMS height implied by w = 2 over the off-road band 0.05-10 cycles/m: σ² = Gd(n0)·n0²·(1/n1 − 1/n2). Using class geometric means: A 1.8 mm, B 3.6, C 7.1, D 14.3, E 28.6, F 57, G 114, H 228 mm. A maintained gravel road "feels" like C-E; F and above are rough tracks. (Own computation from the ISO formula.)
- For a synthetic gravel road, use a two-segment or three-segment PSD (Andrén-style) rather than a single w = 2 line, and add a narrow-band peak around 1.5-3 cycles/m for corrugation (see washboard section) instead of relying on the broadband PSD.
- Because the 10 cycles/m upper limit already reflects tyre enveloping, broadband roughness shorter than ~0.1 m adds little to vehicle response and can be left to render/lidar detail.

### Gaps
- No primary source found reporting fitted Gd(n0) and w for Swedish/Finnish/Australian/USFS gravel or forest roads; Andrén 2006 (Int. J. Vehicle Design 40) piecewise PSD parameters and Cebon's "Handbook of Vehicle-Road Interaction" were not retrieved.
- A published IRI-to-Gd(n0) conversion was not retrieved (several exist in the literature, e.g. by Múčka/Sayers; verify before use).

## (a2) IRI of unpaved roads, and left/right track coherence

### Takeaway
Unpaved roads span roughly IRI 3-15+ m/km (well-maintained gravel ~5-8). IRI is an unreliable descriptor for gravel. Left/right tracks are partially coherent; the isotropic Robson/Kamash model is now considered inaccurate; Bogsjö's single-parameter exponential coherence model is the practical choice, and coherence varies along the road.

### Cited Findings
- Measured gravel IRI: PS-1 6.33, PS-2 7.28, PS-3 5.51 m/km; IRI changed inconsistently with visible deterioration (worst road had the lowest IRI) — "calculated IRI values are not suitable for analysing gravel pavements" — [Žuraulis et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1257077627/34)
- South African TRH 20 IRI vs comfortable speed for a small car: IRI 15 → < 35 km/h; 12.5 → 45; 10 → 60; 7.5 → 80; 5 → > 100 km/h — [TRH 20 Unsealed Roads, v1.5, 2009, Table 19](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- World Bank IRRE scale ~1.9 (very smooth) to 16.6 m/km (very rough); summaries give maintained unpaved ~3.25-10 m/km, uneven unpaved > 7.7, poorly maintained up to ~20-30 m/km — [World Bank Technical Paper 46](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/851131468160775725/pdf/multi-page.pdf); [Sayers et al. range figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Range-of-values-of-international-roughness-index-replotted-from-Sayers-et-al-1986_fig1_275239811) (secondary summaries via search; table not directly read)
- Coherence: ISO 8608 recommends an isotropic assumption, but it is "now broadly accepted" the isotropic surface view (Dodds & Robson; Kamash & Robson 1977/78) is not adequate; Bogsjö validated models on long records and proposed a single-parameter exponential model γ²(n) = exp(−2·b1·n·... ) depending on track width (written γ²_lr(n, ω) = e^{−2 b1 n ρ...}; see paper eq. 1); Múčka found Ammon & Bormann's model most accurate, and both Ammon/Bormann and Bogsjö accurate — [Lamb & Rouillard 2021, Probabilistic Eng. Mech. 66](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/42925/1/2021Synthesizing_dual-track_road_elevation_data.pdf)
- Coherence is nonstationary along the road; b1 and single-track roughness follow (three-parameter) Weibull distributions; data from ~400 km of asphalt roads, track width 1.5 m, IRI 1.5+ m/km; the authors note dual-track coherence for non-asphalt roads remains to be studied — [Lamb & Rouillard 2021](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/42925/1/2021Synthesizing_dual-track_road_elevation_data.pdf); [2020 nonstationary coherence paper](https://vu-9.eprints-hosting.org/40755/1/2020NonstationaryCoherenceCharacteristics_of_DualTrackRoadProfileData.pdf)
- Kamash & Robson isotropy: one profile description yields cross-spectra for any track width — [TRID 74000](https://trid.trb.org/View/74000); [TRID 152042](https://trid.trb.org/View/152042)

### Inferences
- For a full 3D road surface (needed here, since the car can be anywhere across a 3-6 m road), generate a 2D random field rather than two tracks: e.g. a 2D spectral synthesis with a slightly anisotropic spectrum (longer lateral correlation) so that left/right coherence at 1.5 m roughly matches a Bogsjö-type exponential decay. Randomize the amplitude per segment (Weibull, after Lamb & Rouillard) to get nonstationary "good stretch / bad stretch" behaviour.
- Target IRI on a simulated quarter car is a reasonable acceptance check, but given Žuraulis et al., validate with PSD shape too.

### Gaps
- No measured coherence data for gravel or forest roads found.
- Exact Bogsjö formula constants were garbled in text extraction; check the original (Bogsjö 2007, Vehicle System Dynamics).

## (a3) Washboard / corrugation: physics, wavelength, amplitude, speed, placement

### Takeaway
Corrugations are a speed-threshold instability of loose granular surfaces (onset ~1.5 m/s in the lab, "similar to real roads"), with wavelength scaling with speed (field rule: wavelength in cm ≈ modal traffic speed in km/h, i.e. ~0.3-0.9 m), amplitude typically a few cm (USACE severity: < 2.5 cm low, 2.5-7.5 medium, > 7.5 cm high). They form on hills, curves, acceleration/braking zones, intersections and soft spots, in dry weather, on low-plasticity gravel; they drift slowly in the travel direction and loose corrugations disappear on blading while fixed ones persist.

### Cited Findings
- Taberlet, Morris, McElwaine (PRL 99, 068003, 2007): lab rotating-table and 2D DEM; ripples appear above a critical speed vc ≈ **1.5 m/s**, "quite similar to that found for real roads"; ripple height a few mm up to **50 mm** for wavelengths **50-500 mm** (lab scale); strongly asymmetric, steeper face near angle of repose, separated by flat regions; at large v the wheel goes airborne at crests; ripples drift slowly forward; ~200-300 passes for a ripple to move one wavelength; grain size/shape had no effect; heavier wheels → larger amplitude and shorter wavelength; wheel diameter unimportant; compaction and segregation not essential. Scaling model: λ ∝ L = v²/g times a weak function of Fr = (v²/g)·sqrt(ρ/m); amplitude A/L ∝ tan θc — [arXiv 0711.3872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.3872)
- Percier, Manneville, Taberlet (PRE 87, 012203, 2013): plow model; linear stability predicts the most unstable wavelength near onset and vc; vc grows with plow/wheel mass as a power law — [arXiv 1209.5560](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5560); [Taberlet washboard page](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/nicolas.taberlet/washboard/) (ripple wavelength is NOT simply wheel speed × bounce frequency)
- Field rule (southern Africa): corrugation wavelength in **cm ≈ modal vehicle speed in km/h**; mechanism accepted as forced oscillation (Heath & Robinson 1980; Paige-Green 1990): wheel hop kicks back non-cohesive material; "loose" corrugations (loose fine sand crests, removed by blading) vs "fixed" (compacted crests, need cutting/ripping); only fine-grained low-plasticity materials corrugate significantly (but PI up to 9 % seen); seldom form in wet season; roads with modal speed < 20 km/h (mine, forestry haul) usually do not corrugate; grader-caused fixed corrugations at **2.5-3.0 m wavelength, ~45°** to traffic — [TRH 20, 2009](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- USACE TM 5-626: corrugations are ridges perpendicular to traffic at fairly regular intervals; "usually form on hills, on curves, in areas of acceleration or deceleration, or in areas where the road is soft or potholed"; severity by depth: L < 1 in (2.5 cm), M 1-3 in (2.5-7.5 cm), H > 3 in (7.5 cm); measured as surface area — [TM 5-626 Unsurfaced Road Maintenance Management, 1995](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/ARMYCOE/COETM/tm_5_626.pdf)
- FHWA Gravel Roads guide: four causes — driving habits (hard acceleration/braking, intersections), lack of moisture (crust loosens in dry weather), poor gravel (no plastic fines), lack of crown; grader-caused washboard appears at an angle with ridges **2-3 ft (0.6-0.9 m)** apart; transitions from gravel to paved are prone; "can never be completely eliminated" — [FHWA Gravel Roads Construction & Maintenance Guide, 2015](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/construction/pubs/ots15002.pdf)
- Faya, Hardie & Joubert: amplitude ≈ 5 cm, wavelength 30-60 cm — [Dirt road corrugations](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/EJC97374) (via search snippet)
- 2025 review (Nguyen, Tutumluer, Kodikara): well-developed corrugations have amplitude up to a few cm and wavelength several tens of cm; cites Mahgoub et al. 2011 (16 South Dakota sites, measured wavelength/depth) — [Road Materials & Pavement Design 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14680629.2025.2554717) (abstract/snippet only; full text 403)
- Measured gravel PSD break at 2 cycles/m (0.5 m) attributed to corrugation — [Žuraulis et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1257077627/34)
- Earlier mechanism literature: Mather 1962 (Civ. Eng. Pub. Works Rev. 57, 617) cited in Taberlet et al.; TRB 1973 simulation of road corrugation — [TRB HRR 438](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/hrr/1973/438/438-006.pdf)

### Inferences
- Parameterize per segment: λ ≈ v_modal(km/h)/100 m (so 40 km/h → 0.4 m, 80 km/h → 0.8 m on a rally stage's normal-traffic speed, not rally speed), amplitude 1-5 cm peak-to-trough (L/M severity), asymmetric sawtooth-like profile with steep face toward the direction of travel, ridges perpendicular to travel (crests may be slightly curved, and limited to the wheel-path bands rather than the full width).
- Place with a weight field: high before corners and junctions (braking), corner exits and uphill grades (traction), dry/low-crown sections; near zero on very low-speed sections (< 20 km/h, e.g. tight 林道 hairpins) and on wet/shaded sections. Apply a slow amplitude envelope (onset/fade over tens of metres) and random phase jitter of ~5-10 % in λ.
- At 0.3-0.9 m wavelength a collision grid of ≤ 5-10 cm is needed to resolve the waveform (≥ 6-8 samples per wavelength).

### Gaps
- Measured wavelength/amplitude distributions from Mahgoub et al. 2011 and Australian ARRB work not retrieved.
- No data found on corrugation in Japanese forest roads specifically.

## (a4) Potholes, ruts, berms/windrows, crown/cross slope, ditches and cross drains (incl. Japanese 林道)

### Takeaway
Use USACE TM 5-626 severity bins for potholes (diameter < 0.3 to > 1 m, depth 1.5-10+ cm) and ruts (< 2.5, 2.5-7.5, > 7.5 cm) and aggregate berms (< 5, 5-10, > 10 cm); crown 4 % (4-6 % at construction, never > 6 %); flat-bottom ditch with 1V:3H foreslope. Japanese 林業専用道 differ: 3.0 m carriageway, 0.3 m shoulder, design speed 15 km/h, **level cross-section** and no superelevation, drainage by rolling grade and cross drains (rubber water bars dominate), earth side ditch only where needed.

### Cited Findings
- Potholes (TM 5-626): bowl-shaped, usually < 1 m diameter; grow faster when water collects. Severity table (max depth vs average diameter < 0.3 m / 0.3-0.6 / 0.6-1 / > 1 m): depth 1.5-5 cm → L L M M; 5-10 cm → L M H H; > 10 cm → M H H H; potholes > 1 m are counted as area/7 sq ft equivalents — [TM 5-626](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/ARMYCOE/COETM/tm_5_626.pdf)
- Potholes most damaging: 250-1500 mm diameter, deeper than 50-75 mm; worst at the bottom of vertical curves (sags), on level sections with poor camber, and near bridges/culverts; generally absent/reduced on grades; recur at the same location for years — [TRH 20](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- Ruts (TM 5-626): depressions in the wheel path parallel to centerline; L < 2.5 cm, M 2.5-7.5 cm, H > 7.5 cm. TRH 20: ruts form by deformation/ravelling of low-cohesion material; on excessively wide roads ruts form in both directions; regravelling shifts rut positions; on grades ruts become erosion channels — [TM 5-626](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/ARMYCOE/COETM/tm_5_626.pdf); [TRH 20](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- Loose aggregate / berms (TM 5-626): traffic moves loose aggregate out of wheel paths into berms "in the center or along the shoulder"; L berm < 5 cm deep, M 5-10 cm, H > 10 cm. TRH 20: loose gravel concentrates "in windrows between wheel tracks or alongside the travelled portion" — [TM 5-626](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/ARMYCOE/COETM/tm_5_626.pdf); [TRH 20](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- Improper cross section (TM 5-626): L flat (no cross slope), M bowl-shaped, H severe depressions; "no crown is used on curves, because they are usually banked" — [TM 5-626](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/ARMYCOE/COETM/tm_5_626.pdf)
- Crown (FHWA): ~1/2 inch per foot (≈ **4 %**) highly recommended; do not exceed **6 %**; ideal "straight A" shape, parabolic crown is a problem (flat middle → potholes and ruts); excessive crown makes drivers use the middle of the road; finish to ±0.5 % of the typical cross slope; typical section: 4 % crown, foreslope 1V:3H or flatter, never steeper than 1V:2H, flat-bottom ditch recommended, backslope as soil allows; high shoulders ("secondary ditches") trap water along the edge; maintenance windrows at the edge are allowed in some (arid) regions, forbidden in others — [FHWA Gravel Roads Guide 2015](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/construction/pubs/ots15002.pdf)
- Crossfall (TRH 20): recommendations range from 3 % (narrow slippery) to 5 % (straight, ≥ 6 m wide); ~1 % is lost soon after construction, so build at **4-6 %**; in wet areas in-service crossfall ≤ 4 %; many authorities maintain 4 %; superelevation max 5 %; erosion expected with grades or cambers > ~5 % unless protected — [TRH 20](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- Mitre (turn-out) drain spacing (TRH 20 Table 17), erodible material minimum spacing: 50 m at 2 % grade, 40 m at 4 %, 30 m at 6 %, 20 m at 8 %, 10 m at 10-14 % — [TRH 20](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/draftTRH20.15x.pdf)
- USFS Low-Volume Roads Engineering (Keller & Sherar), cross-drain spacing: grade 0-5 % → max 75 m (non-erosive) / 40 m (erosive) ... 16-20 % → 35 m / 15 m; rolling dips suited to 20-50 km/h roads and grades < 12 % — [USFS LVR ch. 7](https://www.fs.usda.gov/t-d/programs/forest_mgmt/projects/lowvolroads/ch7.pdf) (values via search summary; PDF not directly read). Packer-based water bar table: 75 m at 2 %, 40 m at 5 %, 15 m at 20 %, 9 m at 30 %; rolling dips inappropriate above 15 % grade — [Roads for Water](https://roadsforwater.org/guideline/water-harvesting-and-drainage-from-unpaved-roads/use-basic-road-surface-drainage/); rolling dips at 30-45° to alignment — [Napa County BMP](https://www.napacounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/18926/Rolling-Dips)
- Japan, 林業専用道作設指針 (Kochi; Shimane has identical text), based on 林道規程 (1973, 林野道第107号) and 林道技術基準 (1998): design vehicle 10 t truck (L 10.5 m, W 2.5 m, min. turning radius 12 m); carriageway **3.0 m**; shoulder side margin **0.30 m**; design speed **15 km/h**; curve radius ≥ 12 m (≥ 8 m for smaller design vehicle) with widening per table; **no superelevation** (片勾配は設けない); surface is gravel (砂利); **cross slope level (横断勾配は水平)**, surface water dispersed by combining longitudinal grade with cross drains (横断排水工), i.e. rolling grade (波形勾配) plus earthen humps (土構造凹凸) or simple materials; side ditches only where outside water flows in, unlined (素掘り); fill slopes 1:1.2 standard, compacted in ≤ 30 cm lifts; built on slopes ≤ ~30° — [高知県林業専用道作設指針](https://www.pref.kochi.lg.jp/doc/senyoudou/file_contents/file_20215182151026_1.pdf); [島根県林業専用道作設指針](https://www.pref.shimane.lg.jp/industry/norin/ringyo/rindo/index.data/040401sakusetsushishin.pdf)
- Nagasaki forest road detail rules: minimum longitudinal grade 2.0 % on gravel/unlined-ditch sections (0.5 % paved/lined); drainage design safety factor 1.2 for side and cross ditches, 10-year rainfall — [長崎県 資料](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2025/03/1742878009.pdf) (via search summary)
- Tokyo work-road survey: 142 cross drains, 130 (92 %) rubber water bars; unlined (素掘り) ones used where grade ~5° — [東京都農林水産振興財団](https://www.tokyo-aff.or.jp/uploaded/attachment/7259.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Japanese forest roads: model cross slope ≈ 0 % ± local deformation (not 4 % crown), with a rubber water bar or earthen hump every few tens of metres on grades (spacing scaling with grade like the USFS/TRH tables), oblique at ~30-45°, height ~5-15 cm (height not sourced). Rally-use 林道 that are 幹線林道 (often paved or older standards) may differ; check 林道規程 for those.
- Pothole field: Poisson placement with intensity concentrated at sags, flat low-crown sections, near culverts/bridges, in wheel paths; diameter log-normal-ish 0.2-1.0 m, depth 2-10 cm, bowl shape with sharp rim; cluster (enlarging into neighbours). Ruts: two bands at ±(track/2) from the lane's centre line, Gaussian cross-profile ~0.3-0.5 m wide, depth 1-5 cm, with a centre ridge/windrow of loose gravel 2-10 cm, plus edge windrows near the shoulder.
- Edge: road surface → shoulder break → foreslope 1:3 (1:2 max) → ditch (flat bottom) → backslope; the DEM at 5-10 m cannot hold this and should be overridden in a corridor around the OSM centerline.

### Gaps
- No statistical data (density per km, size distributions) for potholes on unpaved roads; TM 5-626 counts per ~230 m² sample unit but gives no typical counts.
- 林道規程 values for 幹線林道 (cross slope, ditch dimensions, cross-drain spacing) not retrieved; the explanatory book 『林道規程－運用と解説－』 (日本林道協会) is the source to check.

## (b) Generation methods

### Takeaway
Standard practice: harmonic superposition (sum of cosines with random phase, amplitudes from the target PSD) or IFFT of the target PSD with random phases; extend to 2D with a 2D spectrum or with a coherence function between tracks; add discrete features (corrugation, potholes, ruts, berms, water bars) as parametric overlays placed by rules; impose nonstationarity by segment-wise amplitude variation.

### Cited Findings
- Harmonic superposition from ISO class (formula in section a1), Δn = 1/L, B = sampling interval, random phase uniform — [Agostinacchio et al. 2014](https://d-nb.info/1100862730/34)
- MATLAB-style implementations use amplitude sqrt(2·Gd·Δn) per component with random phase; a frequent bug is a factor confusion between Gd(n) and Gd(Ω) — [MathWorks Answers 433320](https://mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/433320-why-does-my-iso-roads-doesn-t-fit-to-the-reference-values)
- Dual-track synthesis: generate one track, then the second as correlated part (via coherence γ²) plus incoherent part (1−γ²); vary roughness (Weibull) and coherence parameter b1 (Weibull) per stationary segment to emulate real nonstationarity; demonstrated for a 25 km road — [Lamb & Rouillard 2021](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/42925/1/2021Synthesizing_dual-track_road_elevation_data.pdf)
- Isotropic model: a single-profile description gives cross spectra for any track width (classic Robson/Kamash approach; ISO 8608 recommends isotropy) — [TRID 152042](https://trid.trb.org/View/152042); shown inaccurate by Bogsjö — [Lamb & Rouillard 2021](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/42925/1/2021Synthesizing_dual-track_road_elevation_data.pdf)

### Inferences
- Practical pipeline for several km: (1) build a road-aligned (u, v) grid from the OSM centerline (OpenCRG-like curvilinear frame); (2) macro shape: DEM-derived u-profile, smoothed, with cross section (crown/level, shoulder, ditch) as a function of v; (3) broadband roughness: 2D IFFT synthesis on tiles of e.g. 256-1024 m × road width at 2-5 cm spacing, using a piecewise PSD (D-E class at n0 for gravel, steeper slope beyond ~1 cycle/m), blended with overlap windows between tiles, amplitude modulated per 50-200 m segment; (4) overlays: corrugation (rule-based weight field), ruts and windrows (lateral profile functions), potholes (Poisson, sag-weighted), water bars (at grade-dependent spacing); (5) verify by computing the PSD and a quarter-car IRI of the result.
- Generating "from IRI" is easiest by searching the Gd(n0) that gives the target quarter-car IRI on the synthesized profile (numerical calibration) rather than by a closed-form conversion.

### Gaps
- Cebon's methods and Andrén 2006 parameter tables were not retrieved.

## (c) Representation: OpenCRG, PhysX heightfield vs mesh, resolution, render/lidar consistency, and how games do it

### Takeaway
OpenCRG is the industry format for exactly this: a curvilinear (u, v) grid of z offsets along a reference line, typically cm-scale (examples: u 0.01 m × v 0.10 m). PhysX heightfields cost 4 bytes per sample (16-bit height + 2 material bytes), so a road corridor at 5 cm costs ~32 MB/km per 20 m width; triangle meshes are more flexible (road-aligned, curvilinear) but larger. Omniverse RTX displacement support is limited/off by default, so roughness that the lidar and camera must see consistently should be real geometry; normal maps can carry sub-cm detail for camera only. Games mostly use visible geometry for large features and per-surface parametric bump noise at the contact for small ones.

### Cited Findings
- OpenCRG: originally Daimler's internal CRG format, developed with VIRES since 2008, funded by Audi, BMW, Daimler, Porsche, VW; at ASAM since Nov 2018; v1.2.0 2020-09-30, v2.0.0 2026-01-26; Apache 2.0; C-API and MATLAB tools; used for tyre, vibration and driving simulation; OpenDRIVE references OpenCRG since 2008; data from measurement/laser scans — [Wikipedia OpenCRG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenCRG); [ASAM OpenCRG](https://asam.net/standards/detail/opencrg/)
- OpenCRG format details (spec BS 2.0.1, 2026-05-20): reference line given by a series of low-precision heading angles φ plus high-precision start/end x, y (both given → integration error distributed); u along the reference line with REFERENCE_LINE_INCREMENT (u_inc), v lateral with LONG_SECTION_V_INCREMENT (v_inc), V_RIGHT/V_LEFT borders (or irregular v cuts); slope and banking as start/end values or per-u channels; data formats LRFI/LDFI (ASCII float/double), KRBI (binary 4-byte real, default) / KDBI (8-byte double), big-endian, 80-byte records; NaN allowed only at left/right borders, with GRID_NAN_MODE (keep / 0 / last valid); BORDER_MODE_U/V (error, zero, keep last (default), repeat, mirror) and BORDER_SMOOTH_UBEG/UEND ramps; REFLINE_CONTINUATION (closed track); modifiers SCALE_Z_GRID, SCALE_LENGTH/WIDTH/CURVATURE/SLOPE/BANKING; optional map projection (UTM, GK, TM) and Helmert datum transforms; WGS84 fields informational only. Spec examples include u_inc 0.01 m × v_inc 0.10 m, 31 longitudinal cuts over 3 m width — [ASAM OpenCRG data format](https://asam-ev.github.io/OpenCRG/asamopencrg/latest/specification/06_opencrg_data_format/6_0_Open_crg_data_format.html)
- Altair MotionView (multibody tools) reads CRG roads with u/v increments and z array with optional NaNs — [Altair CRG road](https://2021.help.altair.com/2021/hwdesktop/mv/topics/motionview/road_tools_roads_crg_r.htm)
- PhysX 5 heightfield: sample = 16-bit signed height + two 7-bit material indices (one per cell triangle; high bit of material0 = tessellation/diagonal flag), 32 bits per sample; heightScale, rowScale, columnScale; heights quantized to range/32767; eHOLE material (127) for holes; eNO_BOUNDARY_EDGES for edge-to-edge tiles; no thickness (CCD for fast objects); modifySamples() for runtime edits (re-set geometry on shapes) — [PhysX 5.5.1 Geometry docs](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/docs/Geometry.html); [PxHeightFieldFormat](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/_api_build/structPxHeightFieldFormat.html)
- PhysX triangle mesh: 16- or 32-bit indices, per-triangle materials, BVH34 midphase default (numTrisPerLeaf trades size vs speed), offline cooking recommended, remap table can be suppressed to save memory; eLARGE_TRIANGLE warns of triangles too large for stable simulation; SDF needed only for dynamic meshes — [PhysX 5.5.1 Geometry docs](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/docs/Geometry.html)
- Heightfields trade flexibility for performance and lower memory compared with meshes — [PxHeightField API](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/_api_build/classPxHeightField.html)
- Tyre/resolution: ISO 8608 upper limit 10 cycles/m for on-road vibration due to tyre enveloping; footprint-mean track ~100 mm wide; loose large grains did not affect vehicle response on measured gravel due to enveloping — [ISO 8608 preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/71202/05b2151f255b44928f80acb897fc0c2c/ISO-8608-2016.pdf); [Žuraulis et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1257077627/34)
- Omniverse RTX: OmniSurface "Displacement" input documented as "not supported yet" in RTX real-time; MDL displacement setting /rtx/material/enableMDLDisplacement defaults to False and can slow stage load; RTX 106.1 release notes add vertex displacement driven by MDL displacement functions; Real-Time 2.0 is default from Kit 108 — [Omniverse MDL materials](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/app_view/prod_materials-and-rendering/materials.html); [RTX Common settings](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/materials-and-rendering/latest/rtx-renderer_common.html); [RTX 106.1 release notes](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/materials-and-rendering/latest/rtx-renderer-release-notes/106_1.html); [RTX Real-Time 2.0](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/materials-and-rendering/latest/rtx-renderer_rt.html)
- BeamNG: terrain is a heightmap with configurable squareSize (e.g. 4096² at 0.25 m); decal roads do not flatten terrain, so authors paint the road into the heightmap or replace decal roads with road meshes (Pikes Peak mod used mesh sections for gravel to fix bumpiness); a 0.5 m grid over 16 km² (8000²) can overload physics, so physics grid may be coarser than visual — [BeamNG Road Editor wiki](https://wiki.beamng.com/Road_Editor); [BeamNG terrain resolution thread](https://www.beamng.com/posts/1139436); [Pikes Peak LIDAR edition](https://www.beamng.com/posts/794880/); [BeamNG Mapping Tools thread](https://beamng.com/posts/1935626/) (forum sources)
- rFactor/GTR2 lineage: per-surface procedural bumps in the terrain data file / Specialfx.tec, e.g. RoadBumpAmp ≈ 0.010 m (10 mm) and RoadBumpLen (spacing), track GDB can override; rFactor 2 TDF defines surface reactions per material — [overtake.gg thread](https://overtake.gg/threads/cars-always-proposing-and-how-to-stop-it.251145); [SimWiki GTR2 bumps](https://simwiki.net/wiki/Remove_Unwanted_Car_and_FFB_Bumps_and_Bounces_in_GTR2); [Studio-397 TDF docs](https://docs.studio-397.com/developers-guide/track-development/general-tracks/track-structure/terrain-data-file-tdf) (forum-level evidence; page 404 at time of fetch)
- RBR: all four wheels simulated independently with multibody dynamics, tyre mass/inertia so wheels lift over bumps; roads built with drainage shape into ditches — [Gamereactor history of RBR](https://www.gamereactor.eu/the-history-of-the-richard-burns-rally-1329093/)

### Inferences
- Memory (own arithmetic at 4 B/sample): road corridor 20 m wide × 1 km: at 10 cm → 200 × 10 000 = 2 M samples = 8 MB/km; at 5 cm → 32 MB/km; at 2 cm → 200 MB/km. A 10 km stage at 5 cm ≈ 320 MB of heightfield (plus cooked edge data). A triangle mesh of the same grid (~2 triangles/sample, 12 B/vertex + 3×4 B/triangle index ≈ 36 B/sample before BVH) is roughly ≥ 9x larger; prefer heightfield tiles for the corridor, with a coarse terrain heightfield (0.5-1 m) outside it.
- Heightfields are axis-aligned grids; a winding road needs either many overlapping road-corridor tiles (each 50-200 m, rotated to the local road heading; PhysX allows the shape pose to rotate the heightfield about its up axis) or world-aligned tiles covering the corridor bounding boxes. Joints between tiles need eNO_BOUNDARY_EDGES and identical edge samples. Tile + stream (load tiles within ~200-500 m of the ego) keeps resident memory to tens of MB.
- Resolution: a ~15-20 cm contact patch and the 10 cycles/m enveloping limit mean 5 cm collision spacing resolves everything a tyre responds to (corrugation 0.3-0.9 m gets ≥ 6 samples per wavelength; pothole rims and water-bar ramps get 3-4 samples across the patch). 2 cm is only worth it for sharp edges (rocks, pothole lips), which can instead be meshes. Watch 16-bit quantization: a tile with 30 m height range quantizes to ~0.9 mm — fine; a whole-mountain tile with 1000 m range quantizes to 3 cm — too coarse for corrugation, another reason to tile the corridor separately.
- Consistency: generate one height function z(x, y) (or z(u, v)) and sample it three ways: collision heightfield (5 cm), render mesh (same samples or 2-5 cm, plus normal map from the same function for sub-cm), and lidar map points (sample the render mesh / ray-cast in sim, then the localization map is built from the simulated lidar, not the generator). Avoid render-only displacement since RTX real-time displacement is limited and would desynchronize camera, lidar (RTX lidar ray-traces render geometry) and PhysX. Contact-level noise (rFactor style) is cheap but invisible to lidar/camera; acceptable only for sub-5 mm texture.
- OpenCRG as an interchange/authoring format: export the synthesized corridor as CRG (KRBI floats, u_inc 0.02-0.05 m, v_inc 0.05-0.1 m) for tools like CarMaker/VTD/Altair and for validation; data size ≈ 4 B × (length/u_inc) × (width/v_inc), e.g. 10 km × 8 m at 5 cm × 10 cm ≈ 64 MB.
