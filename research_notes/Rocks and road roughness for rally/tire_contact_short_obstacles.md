# Tire-ground contact models for short obstacles (rocks, potholes, cleats, kerbs, washboard) in real time

Scope: the tire-contact side only. The ego is a PhysX 5 vehicle (Isaac Sim 6.1, omni.physx vehicle, PhysxVehicle* USD schemas), 60 Hz x 3 substeps (180 Hz), several vehicles per world. Today: one raycast per wheel against rigid ground (`src/autoware_isaac_bridge/src/aib/aib/isaac/vehicle.py:137`, `CreateSuspensionLineQueryTypeAttr(PhysxSchema.Tokens.raycast)`).

Ladder, cheapest to richest (what each captures, from the findings below):

| model | enveloping (filtering) | eff. height + slope | front-of-tire impact (early contact on an edge) | radial stiffness / bottoming | lift-off | cost per wheel-step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 raycast (PhysX today) | none | height under the centre only, normal of the hit triangle | no: the step registers when the ray crosses it | none (rigid wheel) | yes (no hit = airborne) | 1 ray |
| cylinder sweep (PhysX `sweep`) | partial (rigid circle, no flattening) | yes, plane from the first hit of the rigid cylinder | yes, edge hit ahead of centre | none | yes | 1 convex sweep, "more expensive" (no number published) |
| multi-point / 4-point (Chrono `FOUR_POINTS`, ACC 5-point) | crude average | averaged plane | partly (front points) | via the tire model's own vertical spring | yes | 4-6 queries |
| disc/area envelope (Chrono `ENVELOPE`, Sui & Hirshey) | yes (area-equivalent) | height from overlap area | yes | via vertical spring | yes | ~103 height lookups |
| tandem elliptical cams (MF-Swift / Schmeitz 3D) | yes, measured-fitted | height, forward slope, eff. rolling radius variation, camber | yes (quasi-static) | radial force law + (MF) bottoming | yes | 5x6 cams typical; ~0.12 ms for 32 wheels x 5 cams in numpy (measured here) |
| rigid ring + cams (MF-Swift) | yes | yes | yes, up to 60-100 Hz | yes | yes | real time on one core per tire class (vendor) |
| FTire / CDTire / BeamNG node-beam / ANCF FEA | full, emergent | emergent | emergent incl. sidewall | emergent, rim contact | yes | FTire RT: ~1 core per tire at 1-4 kHz; BeamNG at 2 kHz; Chrono ANCF ~500x slower than RT (anecdote) |

## PhysX 5 vehicle (vehicle2 / omni.physx): raycast vs sweep, artefacts, cost, USD attributes, wheel collision shapes

### Takeaway
Isaac's vehicle offers exactly two wheel queries, `physxVehicle:suspensionLineQueryType = "raycast" | "sweep"` (default raycast), one per vehicle; the sweep uses a rigid convex cylinder of the wheel's radius/width swept from max compression to max droop, which "reacts earlier" to a step but still has no tire compliance, no enveloping, and no tire vertical stiffness at all. Wheel collision shapes (part of the chassis rigid body) are, in NVIDIA's factory, filtered out against the ground surface, so a rock is either "ground" (seen by the suspension query) or "not ground" (hit rigidly by the chassis/wheel shape, bypassing the suspension) — the choice of collision group decides which.

### Cited Findings
- The Isaac Sim 6.1 schema (omni.usd.schema.physx 110.3.2, local `.../extscache/omni.usd.schema.physx-110.3.2+110.3.0.../plugins/PhysxSchema/resources/schema.usda`, line ~4006) defines `uniform token physxVehicle:suspensionLineQueryType = "raycast"` with `allowedTokens = ["raycast", "sweep"]`, doc: "Collision of the wheels with the ground surface is detected through scene queries along the suspension direction... Raycasts are faster while sweeps can represent the wheel shape better and thus react earlier to ground surface changes." It is a vehicle-level attribute (all wheels of a vehicle share it). — local schema file (Isaac Sim install, `ISAAC_ROOT=/mnt/toshiba/aeon/isaac-sim`); the attribute exists in the published schema docs since at least 104.0 — [omni_usd_schema_physics PhysxVehicleAPI](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_usd_schema_physics/104.0/physx_vehicle_a_p_i_8h_source.html)
- The SDK's `PxVehiclePhysXRoadGeometryQueryType` has eNONE, eRAYCAST, eSWEEP (eNONE = the user writes the road plane). The USD schema exposes only raycast/sweep (no "none"/user plane). — [PhysX 5.5.1 API](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/_api_build/structPxVehiclePhysXRoadGeometryQueryType.html); local schema above
- Raycast: "extends from the top of the wheel at maximum suspension compression" down to max droop. Sweep: a unit-radius, unit-half-width convex cylinder (`PxVehicleUnitCylinderSweepMeshCreate`) scaled by each wheel's radius and half-width, swept against the scene; "generally produces better results but at increased computational cost". The guide gives no per-wheel cost number. — [PhysX 5.5.1 Vehicles guide](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/docs/Vehicles.html); [5.1.3 unit cylinder](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.1.3/_build/physx/latest/function_group__vehicle2_1ga6ba7b0f2421e093002eae1d02123e6c0.html)
- The sweep starts with the wheel at maximum compression and ends at the maximum-droop pose. — [PhysX 5.1.0 vehicle2 function group](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.1.0/_build/physx/latest/function_group__vehicle2_1gaa170054bd9e086df7895eb817cb0d09a.html)
- The result of either query is a single road *plane* plus friction per wheel (`PxVehicleRoadGeometryState`); the component can be replaced by anything that writes plane + friction. — [PhysX 5.5.1 Vehicles guide](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/docs/Vehicles.html)
- Step artefact, NVIDIA's own words: "If the vehicle encounters a sudden change in road geometry, it often happens that the wheel may only be placed on the ground by exceeding the maximum compression of the suspension." Fix in the SDK: clamp at max compression and let `PxVehiclePhysXConstraintComponent` (suspension-limit constraint) push the vehicle up; or small timesteps / a nonlinear bump-stop spring. — [PhysX 5.5.1 Vehicles guide](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/docs/Vehicles.html)
- "Suspension pop": the schema's `physxVehicle:limitSuspensionExpansionVelocity` (default false) doc: "When a hit with the ground is detected, the suspension jounce will be set such that the wheel is placed on the ground. This can result in large changes to jounce within a single simulation frame, if the ground surface has high frequency or if the simulation time step is large. As a result, large damping forces can evolve..." True limits expansion speed, at the price that "the wheel might loose contact with the ground more easily"; applies to all wheels. The SDK guide adds that then "the tire cannot deliver longitudinal or lateral force" and the player may temporarily lose control. — local schema; [PhysX 5.5.1 Vehicles guide](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.1/docs/Vehicles.html)
- The PhysX vehicle wheel has radius, width, mass, MOI, damping — and no tire vertical/radial stiffness attribute (the tire API has lateral/longitudinal/camber stiffness, friction tables, rest load only). The full attribute list of PhysxVehicle{,Wheel,Tire,Suspension,WheelAttachment,SuspensionCompliance}API in 110.3.2 contains no tire radial spring or bottoming parameter. — local schema file
- Legacy (PhysX 3.4/4.x) vehicle had a flag that "improves the behavior when driving over kerbs" by skipping the internal refinement of sweep results (`eDISABLE_INTERNAL_CYLINDER_PLANE_INTERSECTION_TEST`), i.e. kerb behaviour of sweeps was a known issue. Not present in vehicle2. — [PxVehicleWheels.h (legacy)](https://codebrowser.dev/qt6/qtquick3dphysics/src/3rdparty/PhysX/include/vehicle/PxVehicleWheels.h.html)
- Sweep pitfall: geometry that already overlaps the cylinder at the start pose (max compression) — e.g. a kerb or rock beside the wheel — gives an initial-overlap hit; practitioners reject hits with `hadInitialOverlap()` in a post-filter ("not a perfect solution" but helps off-road). Same thread: sweeps "fall through the world" around complex geometry, and a generated wheel collider caused "jerky suspension movement at low speeds"; batched-query memory must be sized for sweeps (out-of-memory with several vehicles). (UE4/PhysX 4 era.) — [Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/implementing-vehicle-wheel-sweeps-for-off-road-game/135211/6); [NVIDIA forum, PhysX 3.4 sweep](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/physx-vehicle-whith-sweep-contact-in-3-4-build/47533)
- Wheel collision shapes vs ground in omni.physx: NVIDIA's vehicle factory creates four collision groups — VehicleChassis (filters GroundQuery), VehicleWheel (filters GroundQuery **and GroundSurface**), VehicleGroundQuery (filters chassis and wheels), GroundSurface (filters itself and wheels). So wheel shapes never collide with the ground surface; the query is the only wheel-ground interaction. `physxVehicleWheelAttachment:collisionGroup` is "a relationship to a PhysicsCollisionGroup ... that defines what the wheel/suspension scene queries should treat as a ground surface". — local `omni.physx.vehicle-110.3.2.../omni/physxvehicle/scripts/helpers/Factory.py` lines 95-126; local schema line ~3246
- aib already follows that pattern (`/World/vehicleCollisionGroups/chassis`, `groundQuery`, attachments' `collisionGroup` -> `groundQuery`). — `src/autoware_isaac_bridge/src/aib/aib/isaac/vehicle.py:95-96,206`
- OmniGraph "Get Wheel State" reports per wheel the ground hit position, ground plane and the PhysX actor hit — usable to log which rock each wheel saw. — [omni.physx.graph Get Wheel State](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/extensions/latest/ext_omnigraph/node-library/nodes/omni-physx-graph/vehiclegetwheelstate-1.html)

### Inferences
- With raycast, a 10 cm rock only exists for a wheel once the centre line reaches it; at 10 m/s and 180 Hz the wheel moves 5.6 cm per substep, so the full rock height appears as a jounce step within 1-2 substeps, at the hub, with the triangle normal of the rock top (or its vertical face if the ray grazes it). Expect exactly the "pop": a damper-force spike, then either max-compression clamping or launch. The real tire meets the edge ~sqrt(2Rh - h^2) ahead of the centre (R=0.33 m, h=0.1 m: ~0.24 m, i.e. ~24 ms earlier at 10 m/s) and envelops it.
- Sweep fixes the timing (edge contact ahead of the centre) but as a *rigid* cylinder: the hit plane jumps from flat to the edge-tangent plane, so it produces a steep effective slope, a large longitudinal (braking) component and a sharp height rise — still harsher than a real tire, and no tire spring to absorb it. It also misses obstacles that only touch the tire sidewall outside the cylinder's width, and picks one plane per wheel (no averaging of two rocks).
- Because the schema lacks "none", the only USD-level way to inject an enveloped/effective surface is to give the wheel queries their *own* collision geometry: put a pre-filtered ("effective") road mesh in the ground-query group and the true rocks in a group the queries ignore (or vice versa). Alternatively drive wheels outside the PhysX vehicle (custom forces), which loses the vehicle SDK. Neither is documented by NVIDIA; both need testing.
- Putting rocks outside the ground group makes them collide with the wheel collision shapes, which are rigid parts of the chassis body: the impact impulse goes straight into the sprung mass with no suspension or tire compliance — the "jumps" will be too violent. Rocks for tires belong in the ground-query group.
- Several vehicles x 4 wheels x 3 substeps x 60 Hz = 720 queries/s per vehicle; a convex sweep is typically a few times a ray (an inference; NVIDIA publishes no figure) and negligible next to rendering, so cost is not the reason to stay on raycast.

### Gaps
- No published per-wheel cost of raycast vs sweep in PhysX 5; should be measured in Isaac (e.g. the vehicle's step time with N vehicles, toggled).
- Whether omni.physx exposes the suspension-limit constraint (`PxVehiclePhysXSuspensionLimitConstraintParams`) or a bump stop; no schema attribute found under that name in 110.3.2. The omni.physx vehicle "Collision Filtering & Materials" page returned 403.
- vehicle2's sweep refinement and multi-hit policy (closest hit vs best-plane) were not documented in the pages read.

## Multi-ray / multi-point contact (games and sims)

### Takeaway
The step above one ray is a handful of probes across the contact patch, averaged into one plane: Chrono's FOUR_POINTS (4 probes at +-0.1 R longitudinally, +-0.3 width laterally, averaged) and Kunos' ACC "5-point" tyre (2 at the front edge, 1 middle, 2 at the back) are the documented examples; ACC adopted it specifically because a single point produced a "rail kerb" effect and sudden steering jolts on kerbs.

### Cited Findings
- Chrono `DiscTerrainCollision4pt`: four reference points `C +- dx*fwd`, `C +- dy*lat` with `dx = 0.1 r`, `dy = 0.3 w`; each intersected with the disc, projected onto the terrain plane; points with non-positive depth discarded; depth and contact point averaged; up to 6 terrain queries per call. Chrono's 1-point version notes it is "only valid for terrains with constant slope". — [Chrono ChTire.cpp](https://github.com/projectchrono/chrono/blob/main/src/chrono_vehicle/wheeled_vehicle/ChTire.cpp)
- ACC v1.0.7 (Aug 2019) replaced its single contact point with five: two contact patches at the front edge of the tyre, one in the middle, two at the back; tyres "climb over kerbs, sausages and bumps more gradually" and the "rail kerb" effect is removed. Kunos physics developer Aristotelis Vasilakos: with one point, a near-vertical kerb edge produced excessive slip angle and a sudden steering jolt. — [OverTake: ACC 5 point tyre model blog](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/assetto-corsa-competizione-the-5-point-tyre-model-blog.171148/); [GTPlanet: ACC 1.0.7](https://gtplanet.net/assetto-corsa-competizione-update-1-0-7)
- A later ACC patch (1.8) improved camber gain over kerbs and bumps. — [racinggames.gg 1.8 notes](https://racinggames.gg/news/assetto-corsa-competizione-update-18-patch-notes-revealed-for-ps5-and-xbox-series-x)
- Note: the 0.1 R longitudinal spacing in Chrono's 4-point is tiny (3.3 cm for R = 0.33 m) — it smooths normals more than it envelops. — derived from [ChTire.cpp](https://github.com/projectchrono/chrono/blob/main/src/chrono_vehicle/wheeled_vehicle/ChTire.cpp)

### Inferences
- In Isaac the multi-probe approach is available from Python (scene-query raycasts) but its result cannot be fed into the PhysX vehicle (no "none" query type in USD; see above). It is useful as a *measurement* of what the PhysX ray misses, or for a wheel model run outside the vehicle SDK.
- Probes placed on the outer circle of the tire (front and rear edge at the loaded radius) give the early-contact timing that ACC wanted; probes near the centre (Chrono) do not.

### Gaps
- ACC's exact point placement, weighting, and whether points carry independent forces or one averaged plane: only forum/blog paraphrase found, no developer technical write-up.

## Enveloping models: MF-Swift tandem cam, Zegelaar/Schmeitz, rigid ring, FTire/CDTire — and whether a tandem cam runs per wheel at 180 Hz in numpy

### Takeaway
The tandem elliptical cam ("tandem-egg") of Zegelaar (1998) and Schmeitz (2004) turns any road profile into an effective height w, forward slope beta_y (and in 3D a camber slope beta_x) and an effective rolling-radius variation, using cams of the shape (x/(a_e R0))^c_e + (y/(b_e R0))^c_e = 1 spaced over a tandem base l_s = 2 p_ls a; it is purely geometric (max-over-contour per cam) and a numpy version for 32 wheels x 5 cams costs ~0.12 ms per step (measured here), i.e. ~2 % of a 5.6 ms 180 Hz budget. It is validated for discrete cleats; validation on rough tracks (Belgian paving, corrugation) is poor for off-road tyres, where only FTire matched.

### Cited Findings
- MF-Swift = Magic Formula + rigid ring + obstacle enveloping; valid to 60-100 Hz and wavelengths > 0.2 m, and "rolling over obstacles". — [Structure and Parameterization of MF-Swift (Schmeitz et al.)](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/structure-and-parameterization-of-mf-swift-a-magic-formula-based-rigid-ring-tire-model/811835525060100096-17623)
- Pacejka's Tire and Vehicle Dynamics ch. 10 covers "The Effective Road Plane Using the 'Cam' Road Feeler Concept" and "The Double-Track Tandem-Cam Road Feeler for Road Camber Variations"; it calls the approach of Bandel et al. (1988), developed by Zegelaar and extended by Schmeitz, "most useful". — [Pacejka ch. 10 (O'Reilly TOC)](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/tire-and-vehicle/9780080970165/xhtml/CHP010.html)
- Schmeitz & Pacejka (IAVSD 2004): a 3D effective road surface description and 3D enveloping model, compared with measurements over several oblique cleats, quasi-static and impacting at high velocity. Companion IJVD 36(2/3):194-215 (2004): rigid ring + elliptical-cam enveloping on a quarter-vehicle. — [TU/e record](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/a-semi-empirical-three-dimensional-tyre-model-for-rolling-over-ar/); [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/info/inarticle.php?artid=5356)
- Cam shape (as implemented in ADAMS' 3D Enveloping Contact): (x_e/(p_ae R0))^p_ce + (y_e/(p_be R0))^p_ce = 1; cams equally spaced across the contact width and along l_s = 2 p_ls a (a = half contact length, a = p_A1 R0 (dz/R0 + p_A2 sqrt(dz/R0))). "Schmeitz has shown that 5 cams along the length and 6 along the width of the contact patch showed the best accuracy-performance ratio for a passenger car tyre." For a large off-road tyre: 15 x 10 cams, p_ls = 0.9, p_ae = 0.72, p_be = 1, p_ce = 2. — [Stallmann et al., U. Pretoria, 2014](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstream/2263/41811/1/Stallmann_Parameterization_2014.pdf)
- Validation (large off-road tyre, ADAMS): over a 50 mm cleat, 3D enveloping reproduced the "lengthening and swallowing" and the free response; FTire was most accurate at the impact. On Belgian paving (3-5 km/h) the 3D Enveloping model gave "unacceptable results" (wide oscillatory force distribution); on parallel corrugation only FTire predicted normal forces; One Point Contact "should be avoided for all ride simulations". Conclusion: enveloping cams suit "discrete obstacles similar to the cleats". — [Stallmann et al. 2014](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstream/2263/41811/1/Stallmann_Parameterization_2014.pdf)
- The enveloping output w, beta_x, beta_y gives radial deflection rho_z = (w - z_a) cos beta_y and radial force F_zrad = q_Fz1 (1 + q_Fz3 beta_x^2) rho_z + q_Fz2 rho_z^2 (Schmeitz 2004), plus a "non-lagging" lateral force from beta_x. — [Rack force estimation over uneven roads (arXiv 2002.01655)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01655)
- Reference test for cleat enveloping: SAE J2731, low-speed enveloping with perpendicular and inclined cleats (force/moment and angular position vs distance). — [SAE J2731](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/J2731_200611)
- FTire real time: 2.7 GHz 12-core Xeon E5, 900 contact elements (1.07 mm x 12.5 mm), 4.0 kHz incl. thermal and wear; real-time mode deactivates extensions and reduces resolution. A 2024 Multimatic/VI-grade setup: 2 x 16-core 3.5 GHz, 1 ms step, "one dedicated core per tire". Hard real time needs special packages. (Vendor/partner material.) — [cosin/VI-grade 2018](https://www.cosin.eu/wp-content/uploads/VI-grade_2018.pdf); [Multimatic 2024](https://www.vi-grade.com/dynatc/attachments-2277-83a7/zps2024-multimatic-enhancing-virtual-ride-quality-subjective-assessment-using-ftire.pdf); [cosin FTire brochure 2021](https://www.cosin.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021_cosin_FTire_Brochure.pdf)
- Own measurement (this host, numpy 1.21.5, a loaded 24-core machine): tandem cam with 140 contour samples per cam (5 mm), 5 cams per wheel, 32 wheels (8 vehicles), road from a 1D heightfield via `np.interp`, w and beta from the end cams: **122 us per evaluation of all 32 wheels** (70 us with an analytic road). Script: `/tmp/claude-1000005/-home-aeon-repos-carla-scenario-bridge/9f5477af-dfc8-4a62-8bd2-a87bf8727051/scratchpad/bench/cam.py`. Cam parameters were illustrative, not fitted. — own benchmark

### Inferences
- A per-wheel tandem cam at 180 Hz is cheap in numpy if the road is a heightfield the Python side can sample. If the road must be probed through PhysX raycasts from Python (one ray per contour sample), cost becomes 100+ rays per cam per substep — then use a coarse cam (e.g. 10-20 rays across the patch) or bake the road into a heightfield once.
- The cam gives quasi-static enveloping; impacts at speed also need the tire's vertical spring and the rigid-ring/wheel mass dynamics (MF-Swift's 60-100 Hz modes). PhysX's rigid wheel has neither, so even a correct effective plane goes into a stiff kinematic placement. Washboard (corrugation) is where cam models were shown to fail on large tyres.
- Practical hybrid for Isaac: pre-filter the *ground-query* surface with the tandem cam offline (an "effective road" mesh in the ground-query group), keep the true geometry for rendering and lidar. This reproduces enveloping, early rise and the effective slope with zero runtime cost, but is speed-independent and loses impact dynamics. Untested inference.

### Gaps
- Exact Schmeitz/Zegelaar basic-function equations and fitted passenger-car cam parameters (Pacejka ch. 10, Schmeitz thesis ch. 4) were not accessible (O'Reilly 403, thesis PDF not found). Some search snippets quoted sample values (ellipse height 0.505 m, length 0.370 m, power 1.5676) without a verifiable source — not used.
- No MF-Swift real-time cost figure from TNO/Siemens was found; CDTire cost not found.

## Project Chrono's tire contact options and FEA/ANCF tires

### Takeaway
Chrono handling tires (Pac89, Pac02, TMeasy, TMsimple, Fiala) choose `SINGLE_POINT` (default), `FOUR_POINTS` or `ENVELOPE` (Sui & Hirshey area-equivalent disc, ~103 height lookups); rigid tires collide with a cylinder or mesh; ANCF/Reissner FEA tires are "the most computationally expensive" — one user saw ~500x slower than real time with 4 FEA tires (on deformable terrain).

### Cited Findings
- `enum CollisionType { SINGLE_POINT, FOUR_POINTS, ENVELOPE }`, default SINGLE_POINT, `SetCollisionType()`. — [Chrono ChTire 8.0](https://api.projectchrono.org/8.0.0/classchrono_1_1vehicle_1_1_ch_tire.html)
- `DiscTerrainCollisionEnvelope`: 101 samples along the disc's horizontal diameter, overlap area between disc bottom contour sqrt(r^2 - x^2) and terrain height, converted to depth by a 90-entry table from area = 0.5 r^2 (alpha - sin alpha); 103 terrain queries per call; the file header says it "uses the collision algorithm of Sui and Hirshey". — [ChTire.cpp](https://github.com/projectchrono/chrono/blob/main/src/chrono_vehicle/wheeled_vehicle/ChTire.cpp)
- Chrono docs: handling tires "normally use single point contact or four point contact (TMeasy)", the envelope method for "ride tests on undulated roads or for obstacle crossing"; TMeasy's complexity "roughly comparable to the Magic Formula based models"; rigid tires' contact geometry "can be as simple as a cylinder or as complex as a 3D triangular mesh"; FEA tires "the most computationally expensive". — [Chrono tire models](https://api.projectchrono.org/wheeled_tire.html)
- Four FEA tires + CRM terrain on one CPU node: ~500x slower than real time, FEA the bottleneck (user report). — [Chrono forum](https://groups.google.com/g/projectchrono/c/Q4YLNDoSB3o)
- Chrono::CRM 2025 uses an ANCF 3423 shell tire with 240 quad elements on a Polaris RZR. — [arXiv 2507.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05643)

### Inferences
- Chrono's ENVELOPE is the cheapest "real" enveloping filter to copy: a 1D area integral per wheel, trivially vectorised. It is a 2D in-plane model (no lateral/camber effect) and gives height only (no forward-slope force), so it captures drop-into-pothole and gradual rock climb but not the braking kick of an edge.

### Gaps
- No measured real-time factor for an ANCF tire on rigid ground.

## Game practice: RBR, Codemasters, Assetto Corsa/ACC, rFactor 2, BeamNG, Gran Turismo, SnowRunner

### Takeaway
Publicly documented practice splits in two: probe-based tires with a separate wheel/tire mass (RBR, ACC's 5 points, rF2's node contact patch) and full soft-body tires (BeamNG, 2 kHz node-beam with pressure). Developer-level technical write-ups on rock/kerb strikes are scarce; most sources are press or forum paraphrase.

### Cited Findings
- RBR (Warthog, 2004): multibody system, potholes, rocks and camber felt through handling; physics lead Eero Piitulainen: jumps/lift over a bump need tyres with a specified mass and inertia; wheels and tyres "took on a life of their own" relative to the chassis. Press-era material, not a technical description of contact. — [Gamereactor RBR history](https://www.gamereactor.eu/the-history-of-the-richard-burns-rally-1329093/); [Race Sim Central RBR](https://bhms.racesimcentral.net/article/142)
- rFactor 2: the tyre is a set of nodes (tool guidance ~33-49 nodes; more nodes = more accuracy, longer table generation), the tyre is a separate simulated body from the rim, and the contact patch is processed in detail. — [Studio 397 TGM Tyre Tool quick start](https://www.studio-397.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/rF2_TGM_TyreTool_QuickStart_V3.pdf); [ISRTV rF2 tyre physics](https://www.isrtv.com/pc-racing/rfactor-2-look-behind-tyre-physics-partially-incorrect/)
- BeamNG pressure wheels: tire and hub built of nodes, beams and triangles; `numRays` spokes per side, default 10, "10 for small basic wheels to 20 for smoothest running", most vanilla wheels 16; radial (side) beams with separate expansion spring/damp; tread, periphery, reinforcement beams; `pressurePSI` (road car 25-35 psi, rock crawler 5-15); `tireSupportBeam` (v0.38) keeps tire nodes off the rim after deflation; tire and hub mesh breaking flags. — [BeamNG wheels docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/sections/wheels/)
- BeamNG physics runs 0.5 ms steps (2000 Hz), 4000 Hz possible via `-physicsfps`. — [BeamNG.tech architecture](https://documentation.beamng.com/beamng_tech/architecture/); [Tight coupling](https://documentation.beamng.com/beamng_tech/tools/cosimulationeditor/tight_coupling/)
- BeamNG developer on forum: collision detection ~30 % of the physics CPU load by itself. — [BeamNG forum](https://www.beamng.com/threads/is-there-a-way-to-turn-up-physics.51381/)
- A proposed rim-bead separation model was judged too costly (~128 extra nodes per car, hundreds of beams). — [BeamNG forum: tire beads](https://www.beamng.com/threads/tire-beads-and-bead-physics.48733/)

### Inferences
- A BeamNG-class tire needs 2 kHz; at 180 Hz the stiff radial beams of a car tire (~200 kN/m on ~10-15 kg of tire nodes) are beyond explicit stability, so it is not an option inside the current PhysX step.
- The common thread of "feels right": the tire meets an edge ahead of the hub (front points / nodes), the hit has a forward-slope force (braking kick, steering jolt on kerbs), and an unsprung mass with its own dynamics produces hop and lift-off.

### Gaps
- Codemasters (Dirt Rally/WRC), Kylotonn (WRC), Gran Turismo, SnowRunner: no technical sources on their tire contact found in this pass. No GDC talk on tire-obstacle contact located.

## Radial tire stiffness, bottoming (rim contact), sharp-edge impact, pinch flat

### Takeaway
Real tires have a radial spring (~200-250 kN/m for a passenger car tire, MF-Tyre `VERTICAL_STIFFNESS`) and, in MF-Tyre/ADAMS models, a separate stiff bottoming spring once the rim reaches the road; PhysX's vehicle has neither, so rim strikes and pinch-like events cannot appear without adding them.

### Cited Findings
- MF-Tyre/MF-Swift reduced inputs include RIM_RADIUS, INFLPRES, FNOMIN, VERTICAL_STIFFNESS. — [Altair MF-Tyre/MF-Swift reduced input](https://help.altair.com/hwdesktop/hwx/topics/motionview/mf-swift_mf-tyre_reduced_tire_input_props_r.htm)
- An example .tir shows VERTICAL_STIFFNESS = 236700 N/m with RIM_RADIUS = 0.1905 m (user-posted file, one example). — [Eng-Tips](https://eng-tips.com/threads/pacejka-coefficents-for-lateral-dynamics.348016)
- ADAMS tire models handle bottoming with nonlinear radial stiffness data extended "into the region of extreme deflection" and a separate spline for bottoming forces. — search snippet of Altair MotionView guide / Adams docs (page itself redirected, not read directly) — [Altair MotionView (archived)](https://2017.help.altair.com/2017/mv/htire_alternative_non-linear_radial_stiffness_model_and_bottoming.htm)
- Schmeitz's radial force law adds a quadratic term (q_Fz2 rho_z^2) and lateral-slope dependence to the linear stiffness. — [arXiv 2002.01655](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01655)
- Shape of the obstacle matters: on bicycle tires an 8 cm-radius "cobble" anvil gave considerably lower force at 15 mm deflection than a flat surface; an 8 mm-radius "pavement lip" anvil was also tested; radial stiffness mostly set by pressure. Pinch flats: the tyre compresses until the rim edge traps the tube against the obstacle, more likely under-inflated or heavily loaded. (Bicycle data; mechanism transfers.) — [Silca tire stiffness part 2](https://silca.cc/blogs/silca/part-2-tire-stiffness-wider-is-stiffer-harsher); [Tioga pinch flat](https://tiogausa.com/acentia/tires/pinchflat.php)
- Severe impact damage of steel-belted radial passenger tyres is studied in SAE 2012-01-0795 (abstract only). — [SAE 2012-01-0795](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/analysis-evaluation-damage-durability-performance-steel-belted-radial-ply-passenger-tires-experienced-severe-impacts-2012-01-0795)
- Dynamic radial stiffness differs from quasi-static and scales with pressure (moped tyre impact tests). — [Sobkowicz et al. 2022](https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-f4ae0b61-617c-40d2-9500-bc0011860721/c/Sobkowicz_Obst_Kurpisz_Glowinski_Experimental_1_2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- In PhysX, the tire spring is effectively in series-less: the suspension spring is the only compliance. A wheel with ~230 kN/m tire stiffness in series with a ~30 kN/m spring would change wheel-hop frequency (~10-15 Hz) and soften edge hits; folding the tire into an "effective" suspension spring loses wheel hop. Modelling it means either adding a tire spring between hub and the PhysX wheel placement (not exposed in USD) or reducing max compression and treating "max compression reached" (the SDK's suspension-limit constraint) as the bottoming event.
- A sidewall-height bottoming check (deflection > section height minus a margin, e.g. ~0.1 m for a 225/45R18-ish tire) can be computed from the effective height alone and used to flag rim strikes / potential damage, even if PhysX's response stays rigid.

### Gaps
- No authoritative passenger-car bottoming stiffness value or MF-Tyre bottoming keyword definitions (e.g. BOTTOM_OFFST, BOTTOM_STIFF) found; the TNO/Siemens MF-Tyre manual was not accessible.
- No measured cleat-test data (force vs time at speed) for a passenger tire retrieved, against which to compare raycast/sweep/cam outputs.
